# Mustard White Rust (Albugo candida) Management Guide (सरसों का सफेद रतुआ / सफेद फफोला रोग)

## Farmer-friendly summary
White Rust (*सफेद रतुआ / सफेद फफोला*), caused by the oomycete pathogen *Albugo candida*, is one of the major yield-limiting fungal diseases of mustard (*Brassica juncea*) across Uttar Pradesh and North India. The disease appears in two distinct stages: a **foliar phase** (chalky-white, raised pustules/blisters on the underside of leaves) and a devastating **systemic floral phase** known as **Staghead (*हिरण सींग / स्टैगहेड*)**, where the flowering stalks and pods swell, twist into grotesque, thickened green structures, and produce zero seeds. Yield losses range from 20% to over 60% when foliar rust combines with staghead malformation.

## When this guidance applies
- **High-Risk Geography**: Western UP, Agra, Mathura, Aligarh, Hathras, Central UP, and Bundelkhand.
- **Vulnerable Stages**: Rosette/branching stage (foliar phase) and flowering/podding stage (staghead phase).
- **Environmental Triggers**:
  - Cool temperatures between 12°C and 18°C.
  - High relative humidity (>80%) accompanied by morning fog, heavy dew, or winter rains in December and January.

## Identification & Diagnostic Symptoms

### 1. Foliar Phase (Leaf Blisters)
- Bright, chalky-white or creamy, raised, circular or irregular blisters (pustules) erupt primarily on the **underside (*निचली सतह*)** of lower leaves.
- The corresponding upper leaf surface displays pale yellow, chlorotic spots.
- The white blisters burst open at maturity, releasing millions of powdery white sporangia that are carried across the field by wind and splash droplets.

### 2. Systemic Floral Phase (The "Staghead" Malformation)
- When the pathogen invades the vascular system during flower bud differentiation, it causes extreme cell enlargement (hypertrophy) and cell division (hyperplasia).
- The inflorescence, petals, and young siliquae become heavily swollen, fleshy, deformed, and twisted into a structure resembling the branched antlers of a deer (**Staghead**).
- Flowers become completely sterile and produce no oilseeds.

## Lookalike Discrimination: White Rust vs. Downy Mildew vs. Powdery Mildew
| Disease | Location on Leaf | Visual Texture & Appearance | Floral Malformation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **White Rust (*Albugo candida*)** | Strictly on **underside** | Raised, smooth, chalky-white pustules/blisters that erupt | Severe floral swelling into green "stagheads" |
| **Downy Mildew (*Hyaloperonospora*)** | Strictly on **underside** | Delicate, cottony greyish-white downy growth | Purplish-brown floral distortion (often co-infects with white rust) |
| **Powdery Mildew (*Erysiphe*)** | Primarily on **upper surface** | White, talcum-powder like superficial floury coating | No staghead; leaves simply dry and drop |

## Recommended Action & IPM Management Protocols

### 1. Cultural & Preventive Sanitation
- **Timely Sowing**: Sow mustard timely between **October 1 and October 20**. Early-sown mustard completes flowering and pod formation before the favorable January weather window for white rust.
- **Varietal Resistance**: Grow moderately resistant varieties such as **Giriraj (DRMRIJ-31)**, **NRCHB-101**, or **RH-749**. Avoid highly susceptible traditional varieties in endemic pockets.
- **Field Sanitation**: Collect and burn infected plant debris and stagheads after harvest to prevent oospores from over-seasoning in the soil.
- **Weed Management**: Destroy wild cruciferous weeds (*Brassica kaber*, *Capsella bursa-pastoris*) around field bunds which act as alternative bridge hosts.

### 2. Chemical Control Protocol (Strictly CIBRC / University Label Verified)
- **Seed Treatment (First Line of Defense)**:
  - Treat seeds before sowing with Apron 35 SD (Metalaxyl) @ 6.0 g/kg seed to protect emerging seedlings from soil-borne oospore infection for the first 30 days.
- **Foliar Spray Schedule**:
  1. **Prophylactic Spray (Before Disease Onset / at First Spot)**:
     - Apply **Mancozeb 75% WP** @ 2.0 g per litre of water (400 g in 200 L water per acre).
  2. **Curative Spray (If White Blisters Spread or Weather Remains Foggy/Humid)**:
     - Apply **Metalaxyl 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP (Ridomil MZ)** @ 2.0 to 2.5 g per litre of water (400–500 g in 200 L water per acre).
     - Alternatively, spray **Copper Oxychloride 50% WP** @ 2.5 g per litre of water.
- **Spray Timing**: Spray on calm, sunny afternoons. Ensure thorough coverage of the underside of leaves. Repeat spray after 12–15 days if cool humid conditions persist.

## Farmer Decision Table
| Field Observation | Diagnostic Stage | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| Isolated white blisters on underside of a few lower leaves | Early Foliar Phase | Apply prophylactic spray of Mancozeb 75% WP @ 2.0 g/L. |
| White pustules spreading rapidly across canopy; weather overcast and foggy | Active Outbreak | Spray systemic fungicide Metalaxyl + Mancozeb (2.5 g/L) immediately. |
| Inflorescence swollen, twisted green, resembling stag antlers | Staghead Phase | Hand-clip and bury infected stagheads to prevent spore dissemination; spray Metalaxyl + Mancozeb to protect healthy flowering stalks. |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Sarson me safed dhabbe dikhne par kaun si dawa spray karni chahiye? (What medicine to spray for white spots in mustard?)**  
  *A*: When chalky-white blisters appear on the leaf underside, spray Mancozeb 75% WP (2 g/litre). If the infection is widespread or floral branches begin to twist, spray Metalaxyl 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP (Ridomil MZ @ 2.5 g/litre).
- **Q: Sarson me phool phoolkar mota kyu ho jata hai? (Why do mustard flowers swell and thicken?)**  
  *A*: This is the "Staghead" phase of White Rust. The fungus invades the flower tissue, causing it to swell into green, sterile, antler-like structures that produce no seed.
