# Potato Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans) Comprehensive Decision Guide (आलू का पछेती झुलसा)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Late Blight (*आलू का पछेती झुलसा*), caused by the aggressive oomycete pathogen *Phytophthora infestans*, is the single most catastrophic disease of potato in the Indo-Gangetic plains of Uttar Pradesh. Under favorable conditions—**cloudy, overcast skies with dense morning fog, persistent dew, and cool temperatures between 12°C and 18°C**—the pathogen can obliterate an entire flourishing potato canopy within 4 to 6 days, leaving fields looking like they were scorched by fire and emitting a distinctive rotting odor. Tubers infected through soil wash develop an irreversible dry brown rot during storage.

## When this guidance applies
- **High-Risk Geography**: Western and Central UP potato belts (Agra, Mathura, Farrukhabad, Kannauj, Aligarh, Hathras, Firozabad, Barabanki).
- **Peak Infection Window**: Late December through January and early February.
- **The "JHULSACAST" Weather Forecasting Rule (ICAR-CPRI)**:
  - If daily temperatures remain between 10°C and 20°C with relative humidity >85% for 48 consecutive hours accompanied by rain, fog, or heavy dew, a Late Blight outbreak is imminent within 7 to 10 days.

## Identification & Diagnostic Symptoms
1. **Foliar Symptoms**:
   - Begins at the tips and margins of lower leaves as water-soaked, irregular, dark brown or purplish-black lesions.
   - Lesions enlarge rapidly and cross major leaf veins without restriction.
2. **The Under-Leaf Cotton Test (*पत्ती की निचली सतह की जांच*)**:
   - On humid, dewy mornings, inspect the underside of freshly infected leaves.
   - You will see a delicate, pale **white cottony ring or downy mildew growth** fringing the margin between healthy green tissue and dead brown tissue.
3. **Stem & Petiole Attack**:
   - Water-soaked black lesions girdle the leaf petioles and main stems, causing entire branches to collapse and snap.
4. **Tuber Symptoms**:
   - Spores washed down into ridges infect tubers, producing irregular, sunken, dry reddish-brown decay extending 5–15 mm into the flesh.

## Lookalikes Triage: Late Blight vs. Frost (Pala) vs. Early Blight
| Feature | Late Blight (*पछेती झुलसा*) | Frost / Cold Injury (*पाला*) | Early Blight (*अगेती झुलसा*) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Under-leaf growth** | White cottony mold visible in morning dew | Absolutely clean; no mold or fungus | Clean brown; no white mold |
| **Lesion Pattern** | Water-soaked, blackish-brown spreading patches | Entire upper exposed leaves scorched dry | Concentric target-board circular rings |
| **Weather Link** | Foggy, humid (>90% RH), cloudy, 12–18°C | Clear sky, sub-zero cold, windless night (<4°C) | Moderate warm weather (24–28°C) |

## Recommended Action & IPM Management Protocols

### 1. Prophylactic (Preventive) Protection (Before Disease Onset)
- **Do NOT wait for symptoms to appear during foggy weather**.
- Apply a contact protective barrier spray of **Mancozeb 75% WP** @ 2.0 to 2.5 g per litre of water (400–500 g in 200 L water per acre) as soon as persistent winter fog or cloudy weather begins in late December.
- Repeat protective spray every 7–10 days if foggy conditions continue.

### 2. Curative Systemic Spray (Upon First Spot Detection)
- If water-soaked lesions or white mildew are spotted on even a single plant in the field, switch immediately to systemic translaminar fungicides:
  1. **Metalaxyl 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP (Ridomil MZ)** @ 2.5 g per litre of water (500 g in 200 L water per acre).
  2. **Cymoxanil 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP (Curzate M8)** @ 2.5 g per litre of water.
  3. **Dimethomorph 50% WP** @ 1.0 g/litre + Mancozeb @ 2.0 g/litre.
- **Application Method**:
  - Use high spray volume (200–250 litres of water per acre) to ensure complete canopy wetting, especially the lower leaves.
  - Spray during midday when leaves have dried of morning dew to ensure fungicide adherence.

### 3. Tuber Protection & Dehaulming
- Maintain high, well-compacted soil ridges (*mitti chadhana*) to prevent zoospores from washing down through soil cracks into developing tubers.
- If blight infection exceeds 20% in the field, **immediately dehaulm (cut and remove vines)**. Wait 10–12 days before digging tubers to allow skin curing and prevent spore contamination on tubers during harvest.

## Farmer Decision Table
| Field Observation | Diagnostic Stage | Action to Take |
|---|---|---|
| Dense winter fog forecasted for 3 days; crop foliage clean | Pre-infection / High Risk | Apply prophylactic contact spray of Mancozeb 75% WP (2.5 g/L). |
| Water-soaked black spots on leaf edges with white mold underneath | Initial Late Blight Outbreak | Spray systemic Metalaxyl + Mancozeb (2.5 g/L) within 24 hours. |
| 25% of canopy blackened; harvest is 2 weeks away | Advanced Outbreak | Cut potato vines (*dehaulming*) immediately. Do not harvest for 12 days. |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Aalu me jhulsa aane se pehle dawa chhidakna kyu zaroori hai? (Why spray before blight appears?)**  
  *A*: Late blight is an explosive water mold. Once it enters the leaf tissue, it destroys millions of cells in 48 hours. Preventive contact fungicides (like Mancozeb) form a toxic shield on the leaf surface that kills the fungal zoospores before they penetrate.
- **Q: Kya pacheti jhulsa aalu ke kando (tubers) ko bhi kharab karta hai? (Does late blight damage tubers?)**  
  *A*: Yes. Rain or furrow irrigation washes spores down from infected leaves into the soil ridges, infecting tuber skins and causing dry brown rotting that ruins the harvest in cold storage. High ridging and dehaulming prevent this.
