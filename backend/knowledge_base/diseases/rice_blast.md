# Rice Blast (Pyricularia oryzae) Comprehensive Decision Guide (धान का ब्लास्ट / गर्दन-तोड़ रोग)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Rice Blast (*धान का ब्लास्ट*), caused by the airborne fungus *Pyricularia oryzae* (teleomorph *Magnaporthe oryzae*), is widely regarded as the most destructive disease of paddy worldwide. In Uttar Pradesh and North India, the disease manifests in three distinct phases: **Leaf Blast** (spindle-shaped lesions with ash-grey centers and brownish-red margins on leaves), **Node Blast** (blackening and rot of stem nodes), and the catastrophic **Neck Blast (*गर्दन-तोड़*)**, where the stem immediately below the flowering panicle turns dark brown/black, cuts off sap flow, and causes entire panicles to become light, erect, white, and completely empty of grain.

## When this guidance applies
- **High-Risk Geography**: Eastern UP, Terai districts (Pilibhit, Lakhimpur, Bahraich, Shravasti), and areas growing fine/basmati varieties (BPT-5204, PB-1121).
- **Vulnerable Stages**: Active tillering (leaf blast) and heading/flowering (neck blast).
- **Environmental & Microclimatic Triggers**:
  - Night temperatures between 19°C and 24°C with high daytime humidity (>90%).
  - Extended periods of leaf wetness (>10 hours of persistent dew or drizzle).
  - Heavy, unbalanced applications of Nitrogen (Urea) without matching Potash.

## Diagnostic Identification & Symptoms
1. **Leaf Blast Phase (*पत्ती का ब्लास्ट*)**:
   - Initial symptoms appear as tiny water-soaked bluish-green flecks.
   - Lesions enlarge into characteristic **spindle-shaped (diamond-shaped / eye-shaped)** spots with sharp pointed ends.
   - Centers of mature spots turn ash-grey or whitish, surrounded by a prominent reddish-brown to dark brown border.
   - Numerous spots coalesce, causing entire leaf blades to scorch and wither.
2. **Neck Blast Phase (*गर्दन-तोड़ रोग*)**:
   - Attacks the panicle base (neck node) just as the panicle emerges from the boot leaf.
   - The neck tissue turns shiny black, rots, and becomes brittle.
   - Result: Panicle breaks or droops. Grains fail to fill and turn into whitish, empty chaff (*chaffy / phulki*), closely resembling stem borer white earhead damage.

## Lookalike Triage: Blast vs. Stem Borer vs. Bacterial Leaf Blight
| Feature | Neck Blast (*गर्दन-तोड़*) | Stem Borer White Earhead (*तना छेदक*) | Bacterial Leaf Blight (BLB) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Symptom on Panicle** | Entire panicle is white and empty | Entire panicle is white and empty | Panicle normal, but leaves dry from tip |
| **Pull Test** | Panicle does **not** pull out easily from base; neck base is black and rotten | Panicle pulls out effortlessly with single finger tug; stem base severed with frass | Leaves show wavy margins; no effect on pulling |
| **Pathogen Type** | Airborne Fungus (*Pyricularia*) | Insect Larva (*Scirpophaga*) | Bacterium (*Xanthomonas*) |

## Recommended Action & IPM Management Protocols

### 1. Cultural & Preventive Sanitation
- **Varietal Resistance**: Plant moderately resistant varieties such as PR-126 or Sarjoo-52 in blast-prone endemic belts. Avoid mono-cropping of highly susceptible fine cultivars (BPT-5204) in humid lowlands.
- **Nitrogen Discipline**: Never apply excessive Urea. Avoid late top dressing after the boot leaf stage. Ensure a minimum of 40 kg K2O/ha is applied to thicken leaf cuticles.
- **Water Drainage**: If leaf blast begins spreading rapidly in the vegetative stage, temporarily drain the standing water from the field for 2 to 3 days to lower canopy humidity and check fungal sporulation.

### 2. Chemical Control Protocol (Strictly CIBRC / University Label Verified)
- **Leaf Blast Trigger**: Apply spray at the first appearance of spindle lesions when 2–5% of leaf area is affected.
- **Neck Blast Trigger**: **Prophylactic spray is essential**. Apply a preventive spray at early heading (when 5–10% panicles have emerged), followed by a second spray 7–10 days later at full flowering.
- **Recommended Fungicides**:
  1. **Tricyclazole 75% WP** @ 0.6 g per litre of water (120 g in 200 L water per acre). *(Gold standard systemic preventative fungicide for blast).*
  2. **Isoprothiolane 40% EC** @ 1.5 ml per litre of water (300 ml in 200 L water per acre).
  3. **Azoxystrobin 18.2% + Difenoconazole 11.4% SC** @ 1.0 ml per litre of water (200 ml in 200 L water per acre).
  4. **Kasugamycin 3% SL** @ 2.0 ml per litre of water.

## Farmer Decision Table
| Field Observation | Diagnostic Stage | Action to Take |
|---|---|---|
| Spindle spots with grey centers on leaves at tillering | Leaf Blast | Immediately spray Tricyclazole 75% WP @ 0.6 g/L. Withhold further urea applications. |
| Crop is at boot leaf / early heading in humid, foggy weather | Pre-Neck Blast window | Apply prophylactic spray of Tricyclazole (0.6 g/L) at 5–10% panicle emergence. |
| White empty panicles observed across the field | Late Neck Blast | Grains already lost. Spraying at this stage will not reverse grain emptiness; note for next season varietal selection and early timing. |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Dhaan me tricyclazole ka chhidkaw kab karna chahiye? (When should Tricyclazole be sprayed in rice?)**  
  *A*: For leaf blast, spray at first detection of spindle-shaped grey spots. For neck blast, the most critical spray is at 5–10% panicle emergence (heading stage). Spraying after the neck has already turned black is ineffective.
- **Q: Kya urea jyada dalne se blast rog badhta hai? (Does excessive urea increase blast?)**  
  *A*: Yes. High doses of Nitrogen produce thin-walled, succulent, soft leaves that the fungal spores penetrate with great ease. Always balance urea with potash.
