# Tomato Early Blight (Alternaria solani) Management Guide (टमाटर का अगेती झुलसा)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Early Blight (*टमाटर का अगेती झुलसा*), caused by the airborne fungus *Alternaria solani*, is the most prevalent foliar disease affecting tomato growers across Uttar Pradesh. Characterized by distinctive **dark brown circular spots containing concentric rings resembling an archery target-board**, the disease begins on the oldest leaves near the soil line and progressively ascends into the upper canopy. Unchecked defoliation exposes developing fruits to direct sunlight, leading to catastrophic **sunscald (*धूप से झुलसना*)** and sunken leathery rot at the fruit stem end.

## When this guidance applies
- **Geography**: All major tomato growing districts of UP (Varanasi, Mirzapur, Meerut, Barabanki, Lucknow, Agra).
- **Favorable Weather**:
  - Warm daytime temperatures between 24°C and 30°C.
  - Alternating periods of high humidity (heavy dew or showers) followed by dry warm sunshine.
  - Crops undergoing fruit-load stress or nitrogen/potassium deficiency.

## Diagnostic Identification & Symptoms
1. **Foliar Target-Board Lesions**:
   - Small, dark brown to black circular or angular spots appear first on the lowest, oldest leaves.
   - As spots expand to 8–15 mm, they develop characteristic **concentric circular rings (*लक्ष्य-पट्टिका जैसे छल्ले / Target Board*)**.
   - Surrounding the brown lesion is a prominent bright yellow chlorotic halo.
   - Heavily infected lower leaves turn completely yellow, wither, and drop, leaving bare lower stems (*lion's tail appearance*).
2. **Stem & Collar Rot**:
   - Dark, elongated, sunken brown lesions develop on the lower main stem near the soil line.
3. **Fruit Damage**:
   - Sunken, dark brown to black, leathery circular lesions develop near the fruit stem (calyx attachment). Concentric rings are often clearly visible on the fruit lesion.

## Lookalikes Discrimination: Early Blight vs. Septoria vs. Late Blight
| Diagnostic Parameter | Early Blight (*Alternaria solani*) | Septoria Leaf Spot (*Septoria lycopersici*) | Late Blight (*Phytophthora infestans*) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spot Pattern** | Large spots (8–15 mm) with **concentric target rings** | Small, numerous circular spots (2–3 mm) with ash-grey center and black dots | Large, irregular, water-soaked blackish-brown patches without rings |
| **White Mildew under leaf** | Absent; leaf surface is clean brown | Absent | **Present**: delicate white downy mold visible in morning dew |
| **Optimal Temperature** | Warm (24°C to 30°C) | Moderate (20°C to 26°C) | Cool (12°C to 18°C) |

## Recommended Action & IPM Management Protocols

### 1. Cultural & Preventative Hygiene
- **Staking & Trellising**: Stake tomato plants on bamboo trellises. Staking lifts the foliage off the wet ground, dramatically reducing soil splash spore transfer.
- **Lower Canopy Pruning (*निचली पत्तियों की छंटाई*)**: Prune off all suckers and leaves within 20–25 cm of the soil surface once plants reach 45 days of age. This improves air circulation and eliminates the initial infection zone.
- **Mulching**: Cover planting beds with black plastic mulch or dry straw to prevent fungal spores surviving in the soil from splashing onto lower leaves during irrigation.
- **Balanced Nutrition**: Ensure adequate Nitrogen and Potassium. Stressed, nutrient-depleted plants are 3 times more vulnerable to Alternaria attack.

### 2. Chemical Control Protocol (Strictly CIBRC / University Label Verified)
- **Preventive Spray (At First Appearance on Lower Leaves)**:
  1. **Mancozeb 75% WP** @ 2.0 to 2.5 g per litre of water (400–500 g in 200 L water per acre).
  2. **Copper Oxychloride 50% WP** @ 2.5 g per litre of water (500 g in 200 L water per acre).
- **Curative Systemic Spray (If Concentric Spots Spread into Mid-Canopy)**:
  1. **Difenoconazole 25% EC** @ 1.0 ml per litre of water (200 ml in 200 L water per acre).
  2. **Azoxystrobin 18.2% + Difenoconazole 11.4% SC** @ 1.0 ml per litre of water.
- **Spraying Protocol**: Spray thoroughly, making sure the lower surface of leaves and the bottom half of the plant canopy are completely coated. Repeat after 10–12 days if warm, humid weather continues.

## Farmer Decision Table
| Field Observation | Diagnostic Stage | Action to Take |
|---|---|---|
| 2–3 lower leaves touching wet soil show circular spots with rings | Initial infection on bottom leaves | Clip and safely destroy infected bottom leaves; apply preventive spray of Mancozeb (2.5 g/L). |
| Concentric target spots spreading into middle foliage; lower leaves dropping | Active vegetative outbreak | Apply systemic spray of Difenoconazole 25% EC (1 ml/L). Ensure plants are properly staked. |
| Dark leathery circular spots near fruit stem attachment | Fruit infection phase | Pick and discard infected fruits; spray Azoxystrobin + Difenoconazole to protect green fruits. |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Tamatar ke ageti jhulsa se bachav ke liye sabse aasan desi tarika kya hai? (Easiest cultural method to prevent tomato early blight?)**  
  *A*: Staking with bamboo sticks and pruning off all leaves within 25 cm of the soil. Since Alternaria spores reside in the soil and splash onto the lowest leaves during watering, pruning lower leaves breaks the infection cycle.
- **Q: Kya tamatar par taamra (copper) ki dawa chhidak sakte hain? (Can copper fungicide be sprayed on tomato?)**  
  *A*: Yes. Copper Oxychloride 50% WP (2.5 g/litre) is an effective preventive fungicide against both Early Blight and Bacterial Spot in tomato.
