# Wheat Yellow Rust (Stripe Rust) Diagnosis & Management (गेहूं का पीला रतुआ)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Yellow Rust (*पीला रतुआ* / Stripe Rust), caused by the fungus *Puccinia striiformis*, is the most destructive foliar disease of wheat in Northern India and the Terai belt of Uttar Pradesh. When humid, cold winter conditions (10°C to 15°C with intermittent drizzle or dense morning fog) prevail in January and February, the fungus produces narrow, parallel lines of bright lemon-yellow pustules on leaf blades. The yellow powdery spores easily wipe off onto fingertips. Left uncontrolled, it destroys photosynthetic leaf tissue and shrinks grain yield by 30% to 70%.

## When this guidance applies
- **High-Risk Geography**: Western UP districts (Saharanpur, Meerut, Muzaffarnagar, Bijnor, Rampur, Moradabad) and the Himalayan foothills/Terai belt (Pilibhit, Lakhimpur Kheri, Bahraich).
- **Vulnerable Months**: Late December to late February.
- **Weather Triggers**:
  - Temperature: 10°C to 15°C (development stops when daily maximum exceeds 25°C).
  - Humidity: High relative humidity (>85%) with persistent dew, fog, or cloudy overcast skies.

## Identification & Diagnostic Triage
- **Visual Symptoms**:
  - Small, bright lemon-yellow uredinial pustules arranged in strictly parallel, continuous narrow stripes along the veins of the leaf blade.
  - In severe outbreaks, pustules also infect the leaf sheath, glumes, and awns.
  - Later in the season (as temperatures rise in March), the yellow pustules turn into dark brownish-black teliospores (*kala ratua* phase).
- **The Finger Rub Test (*उंगली से रगड़कर पहचान*)**:
  - Rub the infected leaf between thumb and index finger. If a bright yellow powder (fungal spores) adheres to your skin like turmeric (*हल्दी*), it is Yellow Rust.
- **Lookalike Discrimination**:
  - *Nitrogen Deficiency*: Leaves turn uniformly pale yellow in a 'V' shape starting from the tip of older lower leaves; **no yellow powder** rubs off on fingers.
  - *Zinc Deficiency*: Rusty brown or bronze irregular blotches appear on 3rd/4th leaves from top; leaf tissue becomes brittle; no powder.

## Recommended Rust-Resistant Varieties
The primary defense against yellow rust is planting certified resistant varieties:
- **Resistant Varieties (Recommended for UP)**: **DBW-187 (Karan Vandana)**, **DBW-222 (Karan Narendra)**, **HD-3086 (Pusa Gautami)**, **WH-1105**.
- **Susceptible Cultivars (Avoid or Scout Vigilantly)**: HD-2967, PBW-343. If HD-2967 is planted, inspect fields every 3 days during January and February.

## Recommended Action & Control Protocols

### 1. Cultural & Prophylactic Measures
- Scout fields systematically once a week from January 1 onwards, focusing on shaded field corners, trees, and near irrigation channels where morning dew lingers longest.
- Avoid excessive application of Nitrogenous fertilizers (excess urea promotes lush soft foliage highly susceptible to rust spore germination).
- Maintain balanced Potassium application (40 kg K2O/ha) to strengthen leaf epidermal cell walls.

### 2. Chemical Management (Strictly under CIBRC / University Label Verification)
If yellow rust pustules are confirmed in small initial foci (patches) in the field:
- **Immediate Spray Window**: Spray at the very first appearance of stripes. Delaying spray by even 5–7 days allows the airborne spores to blanket the entire field.
- **University-Approved Triazole Fungicides**:
  1. **Propiconazole 25% EC** @ 1.0 ml per litre of water (200 ml in 200 litres of water per acre).
  2. **Tebuconazole 25.9% EC** @ 1.0 ml per litre of water (200 ml in 200 litres of water per acre).
- **Application Method**:
  - Use a knapsack sprayer fitted with a hollow cone nozzle.
  - Spray thoroughly to cover both upper and lower leaf surfaces.
  - If disease pressure remains intense and cloudy weather persists, repeat the spray after 12–15 days with an alternate fungicide class.

## Farmer Decision Table
| Field Observation | Confirmation Test | Action Required |
|---|---|---|
| Isolated small circular patch of plants with yellow stripes in field corner | Finger test shows turmeric-like yellow powder | Spot spray the infected patch and a 5-meter boundary buffer immediately with Propiconazole (1 ml/L). |
| Uniform yellowing across entire field on bottom leaves | Finger test shows clean skin, no powder | This is Nitrogen starvation. Top dress urea or spray 2% urea solution. Do not spray fungicide. |
| Warm sunshine with daytime temperature >26°C for 5 consecutive days | Fungal sporulation naturally arrests | Monitor disease progression; hot sunshine naturally suppresses yellow rust spread. |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Kya gehu me peele pan ka matlab hamesha peela ratua hota hai? (Does yellowing always mean yellow rust?)**  
  *A*: No. Only yellowing that forms narrow straight stripes and leaves yellow dust on your fingers is Yellow Rust. Uniform yellowing from the bottom leaves upwards is nitrogen deficiency.
- **Q: Peela ratua dikhte hi kitne din me spray karna chahiye? (How soon should we spray after noticing yellow rust?)**  
  *A*: Spray within 24 to 48 hours of initial detection. Yellow rust spreads via microscopic wind-borne spores that can infect acres of neighboring wheat within 3 to 4 days.
