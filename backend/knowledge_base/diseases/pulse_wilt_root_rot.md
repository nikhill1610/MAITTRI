# Pulse Wilt & Root Rot Complex Management Guide (दलहन उकठा एवं जड़ सड़न रोग प्रबंधन मार्गदर्शिका)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Soilborne fungal diseases—principally **Fusarium Wilt (*उकठा रोग*)**, **Dry Root Rot (*सूखा जड़ सड़न*)**, and **Collar Rot (*कॉलर सड़न*)**—are the single greatest cause of catastrophic stand loss (up to 40–70% mortality) in pulses across Uttar Pradesh and the Bundelkhand region. Because these pathogens survive as resting chlamydospores or sclerotia in the soil for 3 to 6 years, **no standing foliar chemical spray can cure an already wilted plant**. Control requires a strict preventative sequence: resistant varieties, solarized deep summer tillage, organic biocontrol via *Trichoderma*-fortified FYM in soil, and **mandatory chemical or biological seed treatment before sowing**.

---

## Disease Identification & Diagnostic Discriminator Table

Farmers often confuse wilt with root rot, collar rot, or termite damage. Split the stem and check roots before taking action:

| Diagnostic Feature | Fusarium Wilt (*उकठा रोग*) | Dry Root Rot (*सूखा जड़ सड़न*) | Collar Rot (*कॉलर सड़न*) | Termite Damage (*दीमक*) |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Causal Agent** | *Fusarium oxysporum* | *Rhizoctonia bataticola* | *Sclerotium rolfsii* | *Odontotermes obesus* (Insect) |
| **Peak Occurrence Stage** | Seedling (3–4 weeks) & Flowering/Podding | Maturity during sudden post-flowering heat (>30°C) & drought | Early seedling stage (up to 30 DAS) under excess moisture | Any stage under dry soil conditions |
| **Foliage Symptoms** | Sudden drooping of upper rachis, dull green petiole collapse | Leaves turn straw-colored, dry up and remain clinging to stems | Yellowing and lodging at the collar junction | Entire plant wilts suddenly, pulls out effortlessly |
| **Internal Vascular Look** | Split taproot vertically: **distinct dark brown or black pith/xylem vascular streak** | No deep central ring; wood is brittle and dry | Water-soaked rotting at ground line | Hollowed out taproot filled with mud/soil particles |
| **External Root Texture** | Roots intact, external skin looks normal | **Taproot bark shreds easily like fiber; tiny black pin-head sclerotia** visible under bark | White fan-like fungal mycelium & mustard-seed-like sclerotia on stem base | Chewed, severed roots with visible live white worker termites |

## When this guidance applies
1. **Pre-Sowing Preventive Phase**: When selecting certified seeds and planning prophylactic bio-agent seed coating.
2. **Crop Emergence to Pod Fill (15–80 DAS)**: When early patches of wilted or stunted plants appear in black (*Mar/Kabar*) or loam soils.
3. **Post-Harvest Field Sanitation**: Planning multi-year crop rotations to break soil inoculum buildup.

---

## What the farmer should check in the field
1. **Pull 3 symptomatic plants gently**: Check if the taproot is intact, shredded, or severed by termites.
2. **Split the taproot vertically with a clean blade**: Look for dark brown vascular staining running upward into the crown (*Fusarium* signature).
3. **Inspect soil moisture & temperature**: Dry root rot triggers rapidly when soil moisture drops below 15% and daytime temperature exceeds 30°C.
4. **Examine stem base at soil level**: Look for white web-like fungal growth and brown sclerotial beads (*Sclerotium* signature).

## Integrated Management Strategy (IPM & IDM)

### 1. Cultural and Agronomic Management
- **Resistant/Tolerant Cultivars**:
  - *Chickpea*: **Radhey**, **JG-14** (heat and wilt tolerant), **KWR-108**, **IPC-2004-29**, **DCP-92-3**.
  - *Lentil*: **Shekhar-3 (KLS-218)**, **HUL-57**, **IPL-81**, **IPL-316**.
  - *Pigeonpea*: **Narendra Arhar-1**, **Bahar**, **Amar (NDA-2)**.
- **Deep Summer Plowing (*गहरी ग्रीष्मकालीन जुताई*)**: In May–June, invert the soil to expose resting fungal spores and sclerotia to extreme solar heat (soil temperature >45°C).
- **Crop Rotation**: Strictly avoid pulse-after-pulse in wilt-sick plots. Rotate with non-host cereal crops such as sorghum, pearl millet, wheat, or barley for at least 3 seasons.
- **Adjust Sowing Date**: Sowing chickpea when soil temperature cools to 22–25°C (last week of October to first week of November in UP) minimizes early seedling wilt.

### 2. Biological Soil Enrichment & Seed Treatment
- **Soil Application of Trichoderma**:
  - Mix **2.5 kg *Trichoderma viride* 1% WP** or *Trichoderma harzianum* into **100 kg well-decomposed, moist FYM or vermicompost**.
  - Cover with a moist jute bag under shade for 7–10 days to allow sporulation.
  - Broadcast evenly over 1 hectare during final field preparation prior to pre-sowing irrigation (*palewa*).
- **Biological Seed Coating**:
  - Slurry treat certified seed with **10 g/kg seed of *Trichoderma viride* 1% WP** or *Pseudomonas fluorescens* after biofertilizer (Rhizobium + PSB) inoculation.

## Chemical Management & Seed Dressing

> [!WARNING]
> **Chemical Safety Gate (CIBRC / PPQS Compliance)**:
> Chemical recommendation requires current label verification with the Central Insecticides Board & Registration Committee (CIBRC) and local agricultural extension authorities before application.
> Once soilborne wilt symptoms appear on standing pulse plants, foliar chemical fungicide sprays are completely ineffective. All chemical intervention must be applied as **pre-sowing seed dressing**.

### Authoritative Seed Dressing Formulations:
1. **Combination Dressing (Systemic + Contact)**:
   - **Carboxin 37.5% + Thiram 37.5% DS** @ **2.0 to 2.5 g per kg of seed**.
   - *Application*: Dissolve in 10–15 ml water per kg seed, mix thoroughly until uniform pinkish coating forms, shade-dry for 30 minutes before sowing.
2. **Alternative Dual Treatment**:
   - **Carbendazim 50% WP** @ **1.0 g/kg seed** + **Thiram 75% WP** @ **2.0 g/kg seed** (1:2 ratio, total 3 g/kg).
3. **Single Active Seed Treatment**:
   - **Thiophanate Methyl 70% WP** @ **2.0 g per kg of seed**.

*Sequence Protocol*: When using both fungicides and biofertilizers, follow the **FIR Principle** (Fungicide first $\rightarrow$ Insecticide second $\rightarrow$ Rhizobium/PSB biofertilizer last with 2-hour drying intervals).

## Decision Table: Stage-Wise Action Plan

| Crop Stage | Observed Problem | Immediate Remedial Action | Prevention for Next Cycle |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Pre-Sowing** | History of wilt in field | Soil application of *Trichoderma*-fortified FYM (2.5 kg/100 kg FYM/ha). Seed dress with Carboxin + Thiram @ 2.5 g/kg seed. | Switch to resistant varieties (Radhey, JG-14, Shekhar-3). |
| **Seedling (15–30 DAS)** | Isolated wilted plants with vascular browning | Rogue and burn infected plants immediately. Do not flood irrigate (spreads spores). | Ensure drainage channels; avoid excessive early flooding. |
| **Vegetative to Flowering** | Patches wilting under sudden hot dry spell | If bark shreds, Dry Root Rot confirmed: Provide one light life-saving sprinkler irrigation (30–35 mm) to lower soil root temperature. | Conserve soil moisture using Broad Bed Furrow (BBF) or straw mulch. |
| **Podding** | Yellowing, base rotten with white threads | Collar rot confirmed: Scratch soil near collar, improve surface drainage. | Avoid deep sowing (>5–7 cm) in heavy clay (*Kabar/Mar*) soils. |

## Common Farmer Questions (FAQs)

**Q1: Can I spray Bavistin or Mancozeb on standing chickpea plants to stop wilt?**  
*Answer*: **No**. The wilt fungus resides inside the internal xylem vessels and deep in the soil. Foliar sprays cannot reach the vascular root system and will waste farmer money. Rogue out dead plants and protect the remaining crop by avoiding water stress and stopping flood water movement across rows.

**Q2: What is the difference between drought drying and Fusarium wilt?**  
*Answer*: In drought, leaves turn yellow and the entire plot wilts uniformly from margins inwards. In *Fusarium* wilt, individual plants wilt suddenly while still dull green, often adjacent to completely healthy green plants, and the cut root displays a brown-black internal stripe.

**Q3: Can I apply Trichoderma and chemical fungicide together?**  
*Answer*: **Never mix chemical fungicide with *Trichoderma* directly in the same slurry**, as chemical fungicides will kill the beneficial living *Trichoderma* fungus. If both are used: dress seed with chemical fungicide first, allow to dry completely for 24 hours, then apply *Trichoderma* or apply *Trichoderma* strictly through the soil in FYM.
