# Early Blight & Late Blight in Potato & Tomato: Comparative Overview (आलू एवं टमाटर का अगेती व पछेती झुलसा)

## Farmer-friendly summary
"Jhulsa" (*झुलसा / Blight*) is the most commonly reported complaint among potato and tomato farmers across Uttar Pradesh. However, farmers frequently confuse **Early Blight (*अगेती झुलसा*)** with **Late Blight (*पछेती झुलसा*)**, leading to misapplication of fungicides and complete crop failure during epidemics. Although both diseases produce dark foliar spots, they are caused by entirely different organisms with opposite weather triggers and distinct management regimens:
- **Late Blight (*Phytophthora infestans*)**: The most lethal pathogen. Attacks during **cool (12–18°C), cloudy, foggy weather with >90% humidity**. Creates rapidly expanding water-soaked blackish lesions with delicate **white cottony down on the leaf underside** on dewy mornings. Entire fields can turn black and rot within 4–5 days. Consult [Potato Late Blight Dedicated Guide](file:///c:/Users/HP/Desktop/MAITTRI/backend/knowledge_base/diseases/potato_late_blight.md).
- **Early Blight (*Alternaria solani*)**: Attacks under **moderate to warm (24–30°C) conditions with alternating wet and dry periods**. Causes characteristic **target-board concentric circular brown rings** on older bottom leaves. Defoliates plants gradually from the bottom upwards. Consult [Tomato Early Blight Dedicated Guide](file:///c:/Users/HP/Desktop/MAITTRI/backend/knowledge_base/diseases/tomato_early_blight.md).

## Side-by-Side Diagnostic Discrimination Matrix
| Diagnostic Feature | Early Blight (*अगेती झुलसा*) | Late Blight (*पछेती झुलसा*) |
|---|---|---|
| **Causal Organism** | True Fungus (*Alternaria solani*) | Oomycete / Water Mold (*Phytophthora infestans*) |
| **Trigger Weather** | Moderate to warm (24–30°C); alternating dry and moist days | Cool (12–18°C), cloudy, continuous fog, rain drizzle, relative humidity >90% |
| **Lesion Pattern** | Distinct **concentric circular rings** resembling an archery target-board | Irregular, water-soaked, spreading dark brown/purplish-black lesions without rings |
| **Under-leaf Appearance** | Clean brown surface; no white downy growth | **White cottony mold/mildew** visible on the lesion boundary on foggy mornings |
| **Disease Progression** | Slow and progressive; begins strictly on oldest bottom leaves | Explosive; destroys entire healthy canopy in 4 to 6 days under cloudy fog |
| **Tuber / Fruit Damage** | Dark, dry, leathery sunken circular lesions on tubers/fruits | Dry or wet brown-black rotting extending deep into tuber flesh; foul odor |
| **Dedicated Guide** | `diseases/tomato_early_blight.md` | `diseases/potato_late_blight.md` |

## Prophylactic vs. Curative Spray Guidelines
1. **Preventive (Prophylactic) Protection**:
   - Before foggy, cool weather sets in, apply **Mancozeb 75% WP** @ 2.0 to 2.5 g/L or **Copper Oxychloride 50% WP** @ 2.5 g/L. This contact protective barrier prevents spore germination on healthy leaves.
2. **Curative (Systemic) Protection upon Blight Detection**:
   - For **Late Blight**: Switch immediately to systemic oomycete fungicides such as **Metalaxyl 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP (Ridomil MZ)** @ 2.5 g/L or **Cymoxanil 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP** @ 2.5 g/L.
   - For **Early Blight**: Apply **Difenoconazole 25% EC** @ 1.0 ml/L or **Azoxystrobin 23% SC** @ 1.0 ml/L.

## Cultural Hygiene Rules
- Avoid overhead sprinkler irrigation during winter months; sprinkler droplets keep foliage wet for hours, triggering immediate blight sporulation.
- Destroy infected cull piles and diseased volunteer plants around potato cold stores and tomato fields.
