# Rice Sheath Blight (Rhizoctonia solani) Management Guide (धान का शीथ ब्लाइट)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Sheath Blight (*शीथ ब्लाइट*), caused by the soil- and water-borne fungal pathogen *Rhizoctonia solani*, is one of the most widespread and damaging diseases of high-yielding, densely planted paddy in Uttar Pradesh. The disease begins inconspicuously at the water line during the active tillering stage as **oval or oblong greenish-grey lesions with an irregular "snake-skin" banded pattern**. Under warm, humid, and overcast conditions, the fungal mycelium climbs rapidly up the leaf sheaths to the flag leaf, destroying photosynthetic tissue and causing tillers to rot and lodge (*गिर जाना*), inflicting yield losses between 20% and 50%.

## When this guidance applies
- **High-Risk Zones**: All rice belts of UP, especially in fertile irrigated areas where high plant density and heavy nitrogen fertilizers are used.
- **Vulnerable Stage**: Maximum tillering to flowering stage (typically 45–70 days after transplanting).
- **Environmental Triggers**:
  - Warm temperatures between 28°C and 32°C.
  - Very high relative humidity inside the crop canopy (>90%).
  - Dense planting with closed canopy and stagnant flood water.

## Identification & Diagnostic Symptoms
1. **Initial Symptoms (At the Waterline)**:
   - First observed on leaf sheaths just 2 to 5 cm above the water level.
   - Lesions are initially circular, oblong, or elliptical, measuring 10–30 mm long, water-soaked, and pale green or greyish-white.
2. **Advanced Symptoms (Snake-Skin Banding)**:
   - As lesions enlarge, they coalesce and develop an undulating, irregular banded appearance resembling **greenish-grey snake-skin** with dark reddish-brown margins.
   - White cottony fungal mycelia and hard mustard-seed-like dark brown **sclerotia** form on the lesions. Sclerotia drop into water and soil to survive between seasons.
3. **Canopy Damage**:
   - The disease ascends vertically from the lower sheaths to the flag leaf and panicle.
   - Severe infection weakens the lower stem, causing the tillers to buckle and lodge into the standing water.

## Lookalike Discrimination
- **Rice Blast**: Appears primarily on leaf blades as spindle-shaped spots with ash-grey centers, or on the panicle neck. Sheath Blight begins strictly on the lower sheath near the water line.
- **Stem Rot (*Sclerotium oryzae*)**: Lesions are black, hollow out the inner stem culm at water level, and cause lodging, but lack the large, banded snake-skin pattern on the outer sheath.

## Recommended Action & IPM Management Protocols

### 1. Cultural & Agronomic Practices
- **Canopy Ventilation (Alleyways / *गलियारा बनाना*)**:
  - In fields with high plant density, create a 30 cm clear alleyway (by skipping 1 row or bending plants) every 2.5 to 3.0 meters (every 10–12 rows). This allows fresh wind and sunlight into the lower canopy, lowering humidity and arresting upward fungal progression.
- **Nitrogen Optimization**:
  - Avoid excessive and late applications of Urea. Apply Nitrogen in three calibrated splits and ensure adequate basal Potash (40–50 kg K2O/ha).
- **Water Management (Drainage)**:
  - Drain standing water from the field for 2 to 3 days when symptoms first appear on lower sheaths. Adopting Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) prevents persistent moisture around the sheath base.
- **Field Sanitation**:
  - Collect and destroy crop residues from previously infected crops. Deep summer ploughing buries surviving fungal sclerotia.

### 2. Chemical Control Protocol (Strictly CIBRC / University Label Verified)
- **Economic Threshold Level (ETL)**: Initiate spraying when sheath blight lesions are observed on 5% to 10% of hills in the field at the tillering/panicle initiation stage.
- **Targeted Application**: Direct the spray nozzle **strictly towards the lower base and sheaths of the crop canopy**, where the fungus resides. A high spray volume of at least 200 litres of water per acre is required.
- **Recommended Fungicides**:
  1. **Hexaconazole 5% SC** @ 2.0 ml per litre of water (400 ml in 200 L water per acre). *(Widely used and cost-effective triazole).*
  2. **Validamycin 3% L** @ 2.0 to 2.5 ml per litre of water (400–500 ml in 200 L water per acre). *(Targeted antibiotic fungicide specifically active against Rhizoctonia).*
  3. **Thifluzamide 24% SC** @ 0.75 ml per litre of water (150 ml in 200 L water per acre). *(Advanced systemic protection).*
  4. **Azoxystrobin 18.2% + Difenoconazole 11.4% SC** @ 1.0 ml per litre of water (200 ml in 200 L water per acre).

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Sheath blight me spray karte waqt sabse badi galti kya hoti hai? (What is the biggest mistake when spraying for sheath blight?)**  
  *A*: Spraying only the top of the leaves. Because Sheath Blight develops at the bottom of the stem near the water line, top foliar spraying does not reach the fungus. The spray nozzle must be directed downwards into the crop base and lower sheaths with high water volume (200 L/acre).
- **Q: Kya sheath blight ke liye pani nikalna madad karta hai? (Does draining water help control sheath blight?)**  
  *A*: Yes. Stagnant standing water maintains >90% humidity around the stem. Draining the field for 2–3 days dries the lower microclimate, significantly slowing the spread of the fungus.
