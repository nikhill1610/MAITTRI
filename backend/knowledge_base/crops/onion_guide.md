# Onion (*Allium cepa*) Production, Nursery Management, Thrips & Storage Guide (प्याज की उन्नत खेती, थ्रिप्स एवं भंडारण प्रबंधन)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Onion (*प्याज / Allium cepa*) is one of India's most critical commercial vegetable crops, cultivated across Uttar Pradesh primarily in the **Rabi season** (sown October–November, harvested April–May), with growing acreage in **Kharif** to bridge seasonal market deficits. Onion profitability depends heavily on three non-negotiable factors: raising vigorous, disease-free nursery seedlings on raised beds, managing the devastating **Thrips (*Thrips tabaci*)** and **Purple Blotch (*बैंगनी धब्बा रोग*)** complex during bulb development, and performing scientific **field and shade curing** to prevent rots and sprouting in ambient storage.

## Nursery Management & Transplanting

1. **Raised Bed Nursery (*उठी हुई क्यारियां*)**:
   - Prepare beds **1.0 to 1.2 m wide, 15 cm high**, and convenient length with 30 cm drainage pathways between beds.
   - Mix 20–25 kg well-rotted FYM and 50 g *Trichoderma viride* per bed (10 sq.m).
2. **Seed Rate & Seed Dressing**:
   - Seed rate: **8 to 10 kg per hectare** (for transplanting 1 ha main field).
   - Seed dressing: Slurry coat with **Thiram 75% WP @ 2.5 g/kg seed** or **Carbendazim 50% WP @ 2.0 g/kg seed** to prevent damping-off (*Pythium / Rhizoctonia*).
3. **Transplanting Protocol**:
   - Transplant healthy, pencil-thick seedlings aged **45 to 50 days (Rabi)** or **35 to 40 days (Kharif)**.
   - Spacing: **15 cm row-to-row $\times$ 10 cm plant-to-plant** on flat beds or broad bed furrows (BBF).
   - Dip seedling roots for 15 minutes before transplanting in a solution of **Carbendazim 50% WP (1 g/L) + Imidacloprid 17.8% SL (0.5 ml/L)**.

## Balanced Nutrient Management

Onion requires balanced nutrition with special emphasis on **Potassium and Sulphur** for bulb firmness, pungent thiosulfinate oil synthesis, and storage longevity:

| Nutrient | Recommended Dose (kg/ha) | Application Method & Timing |
|:---|:---:|:---|
| **Nitrogen (N)** | **100–120 kg/ha** | - 1/3rd basal at transplanting.<br>- 1/3rd at 30 days after transplanting (DAT).<br>- 1/3rd at 45–50 DAT (bulb initiation). **Stop all Nitrogen after 60 DAT**; late N causes thick necks (*डोंगे*) and rapid rotting in storage. |
| **Phosphorus ($P_2O_5$)** | **50–60 kg/ha** | 100% basal application via Single Super Phosphate (SSP @ 350 kg/ha). |
| **Potassium ($K_2O$)** | **80–100 kg/ha** | 50% basal + 50% at 45 DAT via Muriate of Potash (MOP). Enhances bulb scale thickness and storability. |
| **Sulphur (S)** | **30–40 kg/ha** | Crucial for pungency, bulb size, and peel luster. Applied basal via **Agricultural Gypsum (200–250 kg/ha)** or **Elemental Bentonite Sulphur (30 kg/ha)**. |

## Weed Control in Transplanted Onion

Onion leaves are slender and upright, offering zero canopy cover, making early weed competition disastrous (40–60% yield loss):
- **Pre-Emergence Chemical Herbicide**:
  - **Pendimethalin 30% EC** @ **3.0 to 3.3 L/ha (1.0 kg a.i./ha)** sprayed within 2–3 days after transplanting onto moist soil; OR
  - **Oxyfluorfen 23.5% EC** @ **650–800 ml/ha (150–200 g a.i./ha)** sprayed pre-transplanting or early post-emergence (15–20 DAT) when weeds are at 2–3 leaf stage.
- **Hand Weeding**: One manual hoeing at 40–45 DAT to loosen soil around developing bulbs.

## Major Pests & Diseases Management

### 1. Onion Thrips (*Thrips tabaci - चूसा कीट*)
- **Symptoms**: Tiny, slender yellow-brown insects congregate in leaf axils, rasping epidermal tissues and sucking cell sap. Leaves develop silvery-white patches, curl upwards, and leaf tips dry up ("pencil tip burn").
- **Cultural/IPM**:
  - Install **Blue Sticky Traps** @ 25–30 traps/ha at crop canopy height.
  - Intercrop 2 rows of maize or barrier crops on field borders to block windblown thrips entry.
  - Spray **Neem Oil (Azadirachtin 1500 ppm)** @ 3.0 ml/L at early signs.

### 2. Purple Blotch (*Alternaria porri - बैंगनी धब्बा रोग*)
- **Symptoms**: Small, water-soaked sunken lesions on leaf blades that enlarge into elliptical purplish-brown blotches surrounded by a broad chlorotic yellow zone.
- **Management**: Foliar spray of **Mancozeb 75% WP @ 2.5 g/L** or **Chlorothalonil 75% WP @ 2.0 g/L** on initial spot appearance. For severe infection, spray **Tebuconazole 25.9% EC @ 1.0 ml/L** or **Difenoconazole 25% EC @ 1.0 ml/L**.

> [!WARNING]
> **Chemical Safety Gate (CIBRC / PPQS Compliance)**:
> Chemical recommendation requires current label verification with the Central Insecticides Board & Registration Committee (CIBRC) and local agricultural extension authorities before application.

### CIBRC Approved Formulations for Onion Thrips:
- **Fipronil 5% SC** @ **1.0 to 1.5 ml per liter of water** (1,000–1,500 ml/ha; Waiting Period / PHI: 7 days); OR
- **Spinetoram 11.7% SC** @ **1.0 ml per liter of water**; OR
- **Thiamethoxam 25% WG** @ **0.25 to 0.3 g per liter of water** (100 g/ha).
- *Sticker Requirement*: Always mix an agricultural non-ionic wetting agent/sticker (**0.5 ml/L water**) because waxy onion leaves cause spray droplet runoff.

## Harvesting, Curing & Long-Term Storage

1. **Withholding Irrigation**: Stop irrigation **10 to 15 days before harvest** when 20–25% neck-fall begins. Late irrigation causes bulb cracking and rots.
2. **Harvest Indicator**: Harvest when **50% of the standing crop exhibits natural neck-fall (*टॉपर गिरना*)**. Avoid harvesting immature erect bulbs.
3. **Field Curing (*खेत में सुखाना*)**:
   - Leave harvested bulbs in windrows in the field for **3 to 4 days**, covering bulbs with foliage of adjacent rows to prevent sun-scald (*चिर्रा*).
4. **Shade Curing (*छाया में सुखाना*)**:
   - Transfer to a well-ventilated shade/verandah for **10 to 15 days** until the bulb neck shrinks, becomes papery and dry, and outer scales turn crisp.
5. **De-topping**:
   - Cut foliage leaving **2.0 to 2.5 cm neck** intact above the bulb. Never cut flush to the bulb as it allows black mold (*Aspergillus*) spores into the core.
6. **Scientific Storage Environment**:
   - Store in traditional or bottom-ventilated two-tier bamboo/wire-mesh onion godowns (*कंदा चाळ*).
   - Optimal storage temperature: **25–30°C with 65–70% relative humidity**. Ensure continuous cross-ventilation. Never store in airtight gunny bags.
