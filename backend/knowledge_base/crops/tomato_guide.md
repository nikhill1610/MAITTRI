# Tomato Crop Production & Protected Management Guide (टमाटर की वैज्ञानिक खेती)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Tomato (*टमाटर*) is a premier, high-value commercial vegetable crop in Uttar Pradesh. Reaching profitable yields (200–300 quintals per acre / 500–750 q/ha in indeterminate hybrids) requires four mandatory practices: raising nursery seedlings under a 40-mesh insect net to exclude Whiteflies (which transmit deadly Tomato Leaf Curl Virus), systematic staking on bamboo trellises to keep fruits off wet soil, balanced Potassium and Calcium nutrition to prevent Blossom End Rot and fruit cracking, and pheromone/biocontrol trapping against Tomato Fruit Borer (*Helicoverpa armigera*).

## When this guidance applies
- **Agro-climatic Zones**: Eastern UP, Central UP, Western UP, and Bundelkhand.
- **Seasons in Uttar Pradesh**:
  - *Autumn-Winter Crop*: Nursery in July–August; transplanting in August–September (Harvest: Nov–Feb).
  - *Spring-Summer Crop*: Nursery in November–December (under poly-tunnel); transplanting in January–February (Harvest: April–June).
- **Soil Conditions**: Well-drained sandy loam or loam rich in organic matter; pH 6.0 to 7.0. Poorly drained soil causes catastrophic bacterial wilt and root collar rots.

## Nursery Management & Insect-Net Exclusion
Whiteflies (*Bemisia tabaci*) transmit Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (ToLCV) to young seedlings, causing severe dwarfing and zero fruit set. Protection must begin in the nursery:
1. **Raised Nursery Beds**: Prepare 1.0 m wide, 15 cm raised beds with well-decomposed FYM (10 kg/m²) and Trichoderma (10 g/m²).
2. **Seed Rate**:
   - Commercial Hybrids: 150 to 200 g/ha (60 to 80 g/acre).
   - Open-pollinated Varieties: 400 to 500 g/ha (160 to 200 g/acre).
3. **Seed Treatment**: Treat seed with Thiram 75% WP @ 2.5 g/kg seed + Imidacloprid 70 WS @ 5 g/kg seed to protect against early sucking pests.
4. **40-Mesh Nylon Insect Net (MANDATORY)**:
   - Erect bamboo hoops over the nursery beds and cover with a 40-mesh white nylon net.
   - Keep the net edges buried under soil. This physically blocks whiteflies from feeding on seedlings.
5. **Transplanting Age**: Transplant sturdy 4–5 leaf seedlings when 25–30 days old.

## Transplanting, Spacing & Staking
- **Spacing**:
  - Semi-determinate & Staked Hybrids: 75–90 cm between rows and 45–60 cm between plants.
  - Determinate open cultivars: 60 cm between rows and 45 cm between plants.
- **Staking on Trellises (*स्टेकिंग / सहारा देना*)**:
  - Indeterminate and hybrid tomatoes must be staked 20–25 days after transplanting using bamboo poles (2.0 m height) erected every 3 meters along the row, interconnected with GI wire and plastic twine.
  - *Benefits of Staking*:
    1. Keeps developing fruits elevated off moist soil, reducing ground rots by >80%.
    2. Improves pesticide and sunlight penetration into the canopy.
    3. Increases Grade-A marketable fruit yield by 25–35%.

## Balanced Nutrient Management (NPK + Calcium + Boron)
Tomato requires generous potassium and calcium nutrition for thick walls, firm skin, and deep red lycopene color: **120–150 kg N : 80–100 kg P2O5 : 100–120 kg K2O per hectare** (approx. 50:35:45 kg/acre).

### The Critical Role of Calcium & Boron
- **Blossom End Rot**: Irregular irrigation combined with calcium deficiency causes dark, sunken, leathery dry rot on the bottom (blossom end) of developing green tomatoes.
- *Remediation*: Spray 0.5% Calcium Nitrate (5 g/litre) + 0.1% Boron (1 g/litre) at active flowering and fruit set.

### Fertilizer Application Schedule
1. **Basal Application (at final bed preparation)**:
   - Full dose of Phosphorus: DAP @ 180 kg/ha (72 kg/acre).
   - Half (1/2) Potash: MOP @ 100 kg/ha (40 kg/acre).
   - One-third (1/3rd) Nitrogen: Urea @ 80 kg/ha (32 kg/acre).
2. **First Top Dressing (at Staking, 25–30 DAT)**:
   - Top dress 1/3rd Nitrogen: Neem-coated Urea @ 80 kg/ha (32 kg/acre).
3. **Second Top Dressing (at Peak Fruit Development, 50–55 DAT)**:
   - Top dress remaining 1/3rd Nitrogen: Urea @ 80 kg/ha (32 kg/acre) along with remaining half Potash: MOP @ 100 kg/ha (40 kg/acre).

## Irrigation Scheduling
- Tomato is highly sensitive to moisture fluctuations:
  - *Drip Irrigation*: Best suited (saves 40% water and enables precision fertigation).
  - *Furrow Irrigation*: Irrigate every 5–7 days in summer and 8–10 days in winter.
- **Watering Rule to Prevent Fruit Cracking**: Never give heavy irrigation to a parched, dry field during fruit maturity; sudden turgor pressure ruptures the outer fruit skin (*tamatar phatna*). Maintain uniform, moderate moisture.

## Major Pests & Diseases (IPM Protocols)
- **Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (ToLCV)**:
  - *Symptoms*: Upward rolling, curling, puckering, and severe yellowing of leaves; extreme stunting of plants and flower drop. Transmitted by Whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*).
  - *Management*: Plant resistant varieties like **Kashi Vishesh**; install Yellow Sticky Traps @ 10–12 per acre; spray Neem oil (10,000 ppm) @ 2 ml/L or Thiamethoxam 25% WG @ 0.3 g/L. See `pests/tomato_whitefly_curl.md`.
- **Tomato Fruit Borer (*Helicoverpa armigera*)**:
  - *Damage*: Larva bores circular holes into green and ripe fruits, feeding with half its body inside.
  - *Control*: Install Pheromone traps (Helilure) @ 5/acre. Plant Marigold (*गेंदा*) as a trap crop (1 row of tall African marigold for every 16 rows of tomato). Spray *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) @ 2 g/L or Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC @ 0.3 ml/L.

## Farmer Decision Table
| Field Observation | Likely Cause | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| Black, sunken, leathery flat scar on fruit bottom | Blossom End Rot (Calcium deficit + erratic water) | Maintain uniform soil moisture; spray 0.5% Calcium Nitrate foliar. |
| Leaves curling upwards, thick, leathery, dwarfed | Tomato Leaf Curl Virus (ToLCV) via Whiteflies | Rogue out infected plants; install yellow sticky traps; spray systemic insecticide for whiteflies. |
| Circular holes bored into fruits with larval droppings | Tomato Fruit Borer (*Helicoverpa armigera*) | Hand-pick damaged fruits; install pheromone traps; spray Bt or Chlorantraniliprole. |
| Concentric brown circular rings on lower leaves | Early Blight (*Alternaria solani*) | Remove lower infected foliage; spray Copper Oxychloride 50% WP (2.5 g/L). |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Tamatar me patta modak (leaf curl) se bachav kaise kare? (How to prevent tomato leaf curl?)**  
  *A*: Grow resistant varieties like **Kashi Vishesh** from ICAR-IIVR Varanasi. Cover the nursery with a 40-mesh insect net to block whiteflies. Set up yellow sticky traps (10–12 per acre) in the main field.
- **Q: Tamatar ke neeche ka hissa kala hokar sukh kyu jata hai? (Why does the bottom of the tomato turn black and dry?)**  
  *A*: This is Blossom End Rot caused by calcium deficiency and erratic irrigation intervals. Avoid letting the soil dry out completely, and give a foliar spray of Calcium Nitrate (5 g/L).
