# Tissue Culture Banana (Grand Naine G-9) Commercial Production, Sigatoka & Bunch Care Guide (टिशू कल्चर केला जी-9 उत्पादन एवं सिगाटोका प्रबंधन)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Commercial banana (*केला / Musa acuminata*) cultivation has emerged as a high-income horticultural revolution in Uttar Pradesh, spearheaded by the Central and Eastern plain belt (**Barabanki, Ayodhya, Sitapur, Kushinagar, and Kaushambi**). Utilizing certified disease-free **Tissue Culture Grand Naine (G-9)** plantlets under high-density drip fertigation enables farmers to harvest bunches weighing **28 to 35 kg** yielding 85–100 tonnes/ha within 11–12 months. Maximizing export/mandi grade quality requires strict sanitation against **Sigatoka Leaf Spot (*सिगाटोका पत्ती धब्बा रोग*)**, surveillance against **Panama Wilt TR4 (*पनामा उकठा रोग*)**, regular **de-suckering (*पुत्ती छंटाई*)**, and **bunch spraying, propping, and non-woven polypropylene bagging**.

---

## Planting Season & Planting Material Quality

### 1. Planting Windows:
- **Monsoon Window (Primary)**: **15 July to 31 August**. Highest survival rate, rapid root establishment before winter cooling.
- **Spring Window**: **15 February to 15 March**. Suitable where frost risk is elevated; requires active irrigation.

### 2. Tissue Culture Plantlet Standards:
- Procure certified primary/secondary hardened **Tissue Culture G-9** plantlets from DBT-accredited commercial laboratories or ICAR-CISH/NRCB authorized nurseries.
- Plantlets must be **30–35 cm tall with 5–6 functional dark green leaves** and a well-developed root ball. Reject elongated, spindly, or mottled plantlets (virus risk).

### 3. Spacing & Density:
- Standard High Density: **1.8 m $\times$ 1.5 m** (approx. **3,086 plants/ha** or 1,250 plants/acre).
- Paired Row Spacing: **1.2 m $\times$ 1.2 m in pairs with 2.1 m tractor interspaces** for mechanization and intercropping with green manure.

---

## Pit Preparation & Basal Nutrition

- Dig pits **60 cm $\times$ 60 cm $\times$ 60 cm** one month prior to planting; leave exposed to summer sun for 15 days.
- Refill mixture per pit:
  - Topsoil mixed with **10–15 kg well-rotted FYM or vermicompost**.
  - **250 g Single Super Phosphate (SSP)**.
  - **50 g Neem cake** + **25 g *Trichoderma harzianum***.
  - **10 g Carbofuran 3G or Chlorpyrifos 1.5% DP** to protect against termites and subterranean root nematodes.
- Plant the root ball flush with soil surface; press firmly around the collar without burying the pseudostem crown too deep.

---

## Balanced Nutrition & Drip Fertigation Schedule (Per Plant Doses)

G-9 banana is one of the heaviest nutrient feeders in the vegetable kingdom, removing enormous quantities of Potassium for fruit starch synthesis:

| Crop Phase & Timing | Recommended Fertilizer Nutrients (Per Plant Basis) | Fertigation Application Method |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Vegetative Phase (1 to 4 Months)** | **70 g N + 60 g $P_2O_5$ + 60 g $K_2O$** | 100% P applied basal in pit. Apply N and K through drip weekly using Urea and Muriate of Potash (MOP). |
| **Shooting / Inflorescence (5 to 8 Months)** | **100 g N + 100 g $K_2O$ + 25 g $MgSO_4$** | Heavy Nitrogen and Potassium split weekly. Add Magnesium Sulphate to preserve dark green active chlorophyll. |
| **Bunch Development (9 to 11 Months)** | **30 g N + 140 g $K_2O$** | Taper off Nitrogen; maximize Potassium to drive bunch elongation, finger thickness, and uniform ripening. |
| **Total Annual Intake (Per Plant)** | **200 g N : 60 g $P_2O_5$ : 300 g $K_2O$** | Total 435 g Urea + 375 g SSP + 500 g MOP per plant per year. |

## Critical Cultural Operations (Inter-Culture)

1. **De-Suckering (*पुत्ती निकालना*)**:
   - G-9 produces numerous underground daughter suckers. **Remove all side suckers every 20–25 days** until the bunch shoots. Suckers steal 25% of the mother plant's nutrition and water.
   - Retain only **one single follower sucker** (*फॉलोवर*) at shooting stage (8th month) to raise the succeeding ratoon crop.
2. **Propping (*सहारा देना*)**:
   - Provide sturdy **bamboo or eucalyptus pole support** (or tie dual nylon guy-wires) to plants as soon as the heavy bunch emerges to prevent plant toppling under monsoon wind storms.
3. **Denavelling (*नर फूल तोड़ना*)**:
   - Break off the pendant male flower bud (*दिल/कमल*) 10–15 cm below the last fruit hand once all fertile female fingers have set. This directs 100% of sucrose into finger sizing.
4. **Bunch Bagging (*बंच पर बैग चढ़ाना*)**:
   - Cover developing bunches with **non-woven spun-bond polypropylene blue or white breathable bags (17 gsm)** with bottom ventilation holes immediately after spray treatment.
   - Bagging increases finger weight by 15–20%, eliminates thrips scratches, prevents sunscald, and produces spotless, export-grade parrot-green fruit skins.

## Major Diseases & Integrated Management

### 1. Sigatoka Leaf Spot (*Pseudocercospora musae / P. fijiensis - सिगाटोका*)
- **Symptoms**: Small, spindle-shaped yellowish streaks appear parallel to veins on third and fourth leaves during warm, humid monsoon months (July–October). Streaks turn dark reddish-brown to black, develop sunken grey centers with yellow chlorotic halos, and coalesce to scorch the entire leaf blade.
- **Economic Impact**: Destroys functional photosynthetic leaf area. If fewer than **8 healthy leaves** remain at bunch emergence, bunches fail to fill and ripen prematurely into sour, unmarketable fingers.
- **Control Protocol**:
  - Prune and burn heavily infected, dry hanging leaves (*डी-लीफिंग*).
  - Spray **Propiconazole 25% EC @ 1.0 ml/L** mixed with **1% Agricultural Mineral Oil (Petroleum spray oil @ 10 ml/L)**. The mineral oil acts as a penetrant and adjuvant, doubling fungicidal efficacy.
  - Alternate with **Azoxystrobin 23% SC @ 1.0 ml/L** or **Mancozeb 75% WP @ 2.5 g/L** at 20-day intervals.

### 2. Panama Wilt (*Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. cubense TR4 - पनामा उकठा*)
- **Alert**: Tropical Race 4 (TR4) attacks Cavendish/G-9 cultivars.
- **Symptoms**: Lower leaves turn bright yellow, collapse at petiole-pseudostem junction, and hang down like a skirt. Splitting pseudostem reveals reddish-brown to black vascular discoloration in the outer sheath.
- **Biosecurity Protocol**:
  - Never introduce uncertified suckers or soil from infected regions (Katihar, Maharajganj).
  - Strictly rogue out and incinerate infected clumps; drench pit with **2% Bleaching powder solution**.

## Harvesting & Post-Harvest Handling

1. **Harvest Stage**: Harvest when fingers are **75–80% mature** (angles become rounded/plump, ridges disappear).
2. **Harvest Method**: Cut the peduncle cleanly leaving **30–40 cm stalk handle** for carrying. Never drop bunches on bare soil.
3. **De-handing & Washing**: Separate individual hands using a curved de-handing knife, submerge in clean water containing **0.1% Alum (phitkari)** to coagulate latex staining, shade-dry, and pack in ventilated corrugated fibreboard (CFB) boxes.
