# Groundnut (*Arachis hypogaea*) Production, Pegging Gypsum & Collar Rot Management Guide (मूंगफली की उन्नत खेती, जिप्सम एवं कॉलर रॉट प्रबंधन)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Groundnut (*मूंगफली / Arachis hypogaea*) is a premier oilseed and food legume cultivated across sandy-loam tracts of Uttar Pradesh (Mainpuri, Hardoi, Budaun, Unnao, and Bundelkhand). Because groundnut flowers aerially while developing its pods underground (geocarpy), soil physical friability and calcium availability in the fruiting zone (top 5–8 cm) are the two single greatest drivers of kernel yield. Without adequate **calcium at pegging (*सुई बनते समय*)**, pods form empty shells without kernels—a disorder known as **"Pops" (*पोप्स / खोखली फली*)**. Maximizing pod yields at 25–30 quintals/ha requires sandy friable soil, timely application of **Agricultural Gypsum (250–400 kg/ha)**, subterranean protection against the **White Grub (*सफेद लट*)**, and strict prevention of **Collar Rot (*कॉलर रॉट*)** and carcinogenic **Aflatoxin** mold contamination.

---

## Varietal Selection, Sowing Window & Seed Rate

### 1. Recommended High-Yielding Varieties:
- **Kaushal (G-201)**: Proven Virginia bunch variety for UP; high shell-out percentage (72%), tolerant to leaf spot.
- **TG-37A**: Trombay semi-dwarf, early maturity (100–105 days), high kernel weight (100-kernel weight 42–45 g).
- **Girnar-4 & Girnar-5**: India's first **High-Oleic acid groundnuts (80% oleic acid)** developed by ICAR-DGR; 5-fold longer shelf-life and premium export pricing.
- **Kadiri-6 (K-6)** & **DH-86**: Drought tolerant, excellent for light sandy soils of Bundelkhand and central UP.

### 2. Sowing Windows:
- **Kharif Season**: **20 June to 15 July** with onset of monsoon.
- **Summer / Zaid Season**: **15 February to 15 March** (under assured tube-well / sprinkler irrigation; delivers higher yields of 30–35 q/ha free of monsoon leaf spot epidemics).

### 3. Seed Rate & Shelling Protocol:
- Shell pods **not more than 10–14 days before sowing** to preserve embryo viability. Discard cracked, wrinkled, or discolored seeds.
- Seed Rate: **80 to 90 kg kernels/ha** for bunch varieties; **70 to 75 kg kernels/ha** for spreading varieties.
- Spacing: **30 cm row-to-row $\times$ 10 cm plant-to-plant** at a shallow depth of **4 to 5 cm**. Sowing deeper than 6 cm severely impairs hypocotyl emergence.

---

## Seed Treatment Protocol (FIR Principle)

Groundnut seed contains high oil and protein, making it extraordinarily vulnerable to soilborne fungi and seed rot:
1. **Fungicide Seed Coating**: Treat shelled kernels with **Tebuconazole 2% DS @ 1.5 g/kg seed** OR **Carboxin 37.5% + Thiram 37.5% DS @ 2.0 g/kg seed**.
2. **Insecticide Seed Dressing (White Grub Shield)**: Coat treated seeds with **Chlorpyrifos 20% EC @ 20–25 ml/kg seed** OR **Imidacloprid 17.8% SL @ 2.0 ml/kg seed**.
3. **Bio-Inoculation**: Inoculate with species-specific **Rhizobium culture (*Arachis* strain) @ 5 g/kg seed** + **PSB @ 5 g/kg seed** just before drilling.

## Balanced Nutrition & The Gypsum Pegging Rule

Groundnut requires light starter Nitrogen, generous Phosphorus, and mandatory **Calcium and Sulphur**:

| Nutrient | Recommended Dose (kg/ha) | Application Method & Scientific Importance |
|:---|:---:|:---|
| **Nitrogen (N)** | **20 kg/ha (Starter)** | Basal application at sowing. Symbiotic root nodules fix 70% of crop N from 25 DAS onward. |
| **Phosphorus ($P_2O_5$)** | **40–50 kg/ha** | 100% basal via **Single Super Phosphate (SSP @ 250–300 kg/ha)**. Never use DAP if gypsum is unavailable, as SSP provides free calcium and sulphur. |
| **Potassium ($K_2O$)** | **40 kg/ha** | Basal via MOP (67 kg/ha) for root nodule health and oil synthesis. |
| **Agricultural Gypsum ($CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$)** | **250–400 kg/ha** | **Mandatory at Pegging Stage (40–45 DAS)**: Groundnut pegs cannot draw calcium from root transport; developing pods absorb calcium directly from surrounding moist soil. Band-place gypsum close to plant rows and lightly incorporate (*गुड़ाई*). Prevents "Pops" (empty pods) and pod rot. |
| **Boron** | **1.0 kg/ha (Borax 10 kg/ha)** | Basal or foliar spray of 0.1% Solubor at flowering. Eliminates internal kernel cavity defect ("Hollow Heart"). |

## Major Pests & Diseases Management

### 1. White Grub (*Holotrichia consanguinea - सफेद लट / गोबर का कीड़ा*)
- **Symptoms**: Larvae live in root zone soil and chew primary taproots and developing pods. Plants wilt in circular patches throughout July–August and can be lifted with zero root resistance.
- **Integrated Control**:
  - Deep summer plowing to expose pupae to predatory birds.
  - Install light traps or pheromone traps (*methoxy-benzene*) at first pre-monsoon showers to trap adult beetles emerging from neem/khejri trees.
  - Soil application of **Chlorpyrifos 20% EC @ 4.0 L/ha** mixed in 250 kg moist sand applied along plant rows before light irrigation.

### 2. Collar Rot / Crown Rot (*Aspergillus niger - कॉलर सड़न*)
- **Symptoms**: Circular dark brown lesions develop on seedling stems at the soil collar within 30 DAS. Tissues soften, rot, and collapse covered by thick masses of soot-black fungal spores.
- **Management**: Mandatory seed treatment with Tebuconazole 2% DS (1.5 g/kg); apply *Trichoderma harzianum* (2.5 kg/ha in 100 kg FYM) basal into the soil.

### 3. Tikka Disease / Leaf Spot (*Cercospora personata / C. arachidicola*)
- **Symptoms**: Dark brown to black circular leaf spots surrounded by prominent yellow chlorotic halos on both leaf surfaces, causing severe premature leaf drop.
- **Foliar Spray**: Spray **Mancozeb 75% WP @ 2.0 g/L** on early spots; follow with **Hexaconazole 5% SC @ 2.0 ml/L** or **Tebuconazole 25.9% EC @ 1.0 ml/L** at 15-day intervals.

## Aflatoxin Prevention in Post-Harvest Storage

Aflatoxin is a highly toxic, carcinogenic mycotoxin produced by the fungus ***Aspergillus flavus*** in groundnut kernels:
1. **End-Season Irrigation**: Avoid severe soil moisture stress during the final 15 days before harvest, as cracked soil allows *A. flavus* spores direct entry into pod shells.
2. **Pod Inversion Drying**: Immediately after lifting/digging plants, invert the vines in field rows with pods facing upward exposed to sunshine for **3 to 4 days** until pod moisture drops below 10%.
3. **Safe Storage**: Discard mechanically pod-damaged or shrivelled kernels. Store pods only when kernel moisture is strictly **below 8%**. Store in moisture-proof HDPE bags stacked on wooden pallets 30 cm above godown floors.
