# Bundelkhand Chickpea & Lentil Production, Moisture Conservation & Wilt Management Guide (बुंदेलखंड चना एवं मसूर उत्पादन मार्गदर्शिका)

## Farmer-friendly summary
The seven districts of **Bundelkhand (Jhansi, Lalitpur, Jalaun, Hamirpur, Mahoba, Banda, and Chitrakoot)** represent the primary pulse heartland of Uttar Pradesh. In this semi-arid rainfed agro-ecosystem, **Chickpea (*चना / Cicer arietinum*)** and **Lentil (*मसूर / Lens culinaris*)** are cultivated predominantly on residual soil moisture in unique soil landscapes—deep cracking black soils (*Mar* and *Kabar*) and red gravelly soils (*Parwa* and *Rakar*). Farmers face three severe structural challenges: **soil moisture deficit / terminal heat drought**, the devastating **Fusarium Wilt complex (*उकठा रोग*)**, and the **Gram Pod Borer (*फली छेदक / Helicoverpa*)**. Adopting certified wilt-resistant varieties, **dual seed inoculation (Rhizobium + PSB)**, moisture-conserving Broad Bed Furrow (BBF) planting, and timely integrated protection stabilizes pulse yields at 18–22 quintals/ha under rainfed constraints.

---

## 1. Agro-Climatic Suitability & Soil Characterization in Bundelkhand

| Soil Type (Local Name) | Physical & Chemical Properties | Recommended Pulse Crop & Moisture Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Mar (*मार*)** | Deep black cotton soil; high montmorillonite clay (>50%); deep cracks when dry; high water holding capacity; prone to waterlogging if over-irrigated. | **Chickpea (Desi & Kabuli)**. Sow early on receding monsoon moisture. Never flood irrigate. Practice ridge/BBF sowing. |
| **Kabar (*काबर*)** | Extremely adhesive clayey black soil; highly sticky when wet, rock-hard upon drying; narrow moisture workability window (*बतर*). | **Chickpea & Lentil**. Plowing and sowing must be completed within 4–6 days of reaching *tar-battar* to avoid soil hardening. |
| **Parwa (*परवा*)** | Light brownish-grey to yellow-red sandy loam; moderate fertility; good drainage; lower water retention. | **Lentil (Masoor) & Chickpea**. Ideal for limited 1–2 supplemental sprinkler irrigations. |
| **Rakar (*राकर*)** | Shallow, highly eroded, stony/gravelly red hill slope soil; low organic carbon (<0.3%); rapid drainage. | Short-duration, drought-hardy **Lentil (HUL-57)** or small-seeded Desi Chana. Requires organic FYM/manure. |

## 2. High-Yielding & Disease-Resistant Varieties for Bundelkhand & UP

### A. Chickpea (Gram / *चना*)
1. **JG-14**: Semi-spreading desi variety; **exceptional terminal heat tolerance**; matures in 110–115 days; immune to wilt; ideal for late-sown conditions following paddy; yield 18–20 q/ha.
2. **Radhey**: Highly popular in Central UP and Bundelkhand; bold yellow-brown grains; matures in 125–130 days; tolerant to wilt; yield 20–22 q/ha.
3. **KWR-108**: Developed by CSAU Kanpur; highly adapted to rainfed Bundelkhand tracts; medium bold grain; resistant to wilt; yield 18–22 q/ha.
4. **IPC-2004-29**: Released by ICAR-IIPR Kanpur; high branch vigor; exceptional resistance to Fusarium wilt and dry root rot; yield 22–24 q/ha.
5. **Ujjawal (IPCK 2004-29) - Kabuli**: Extra bold white grain (100-seed weight >38 g); fetch premium mandi price; requires at least one assured irrigation; yield 18–20 q/ha.

### B. Lentil (Masoor / *मसूर*)
1. **Shekhar-3 (KLS-218)**: High-yielding small-seeded variety released for UP; resistant to wilt and rust; matures in 120–125 days; yield 16–18 q/ha.
2. **IPL-81**: Small-seeded, early maturing (110–115 days); ideal for rainfed *Mar/Kabar* soils under terminal moisture stress; yield 15–18 q/ha.
3. **HUL-57**: Matures in 115–120 days; small bold red cotyledon; highly resistant to vascular wilt; ideal for both sole and mixed cropping; yield 16–20 q/ha.

## 3. Seed Rate, Sowing Window & The Dual Inoculation Protocol

### A. Sowing Window & Plant Density
- **Optimal Sowing Window**: **October 15 to November 10**. Sowing after November 25 forces flowering into late February heatwaves, reducing pod-set by 30–45%.
- **Seed Rate & Row Spacing**:
  - **Desi Chickpea**: 75–80 kg/ha (30–32 kg/acre); Row-to-row 30 cm, plant-to-plant 10 cm; depth 6–8 cm into moist soil layer.
  - **Kabuli Chickpea**: 100–120 kg/ha (40–48 kg/acre); Row-to-row 40 cm, plant-to-plant 10 cm.
  - **Lentil (Masoor)**: 35–40 kg/ha (15–16 kg/acre); Row-to-row 25 cm, plant-to-plant 5 cm; depth 3–4 cm.

### B. The Mandatory 3-Step Seed Treatment & Bio-Inoculation
Pulse seeds must be treated in this precise sequence (Fungicide $\rightarrow$ Insecticide $\rightarrow$ Biofertilizer):
1. **Step 1: Biological / Chemical Fungicidal Coating**:
   - Treat 1 kg seed with **Trichoderma viride (1% WP) @ 8–10 g/kg** OR **Carbendazim 12% + Mancozeb 63% WP @ 2.0 g/kg** seed. Shields germinating radicle from soil-borne *Fusarium* spores.
2. **Step 2: Dual Biofertilizer Inoculation (Rhizobium + PSB)**:
   - Prepare a sticky jaggery (*gur*) slurry by boiling 50 g jaggery in 250 ml water, allow to cool.
   - Mix **Mesorhizobium ciceri culture (for Chana)** or **Rhizobium leguminosarum (for Masoor)** @ 20 g/kg seed + **Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB) @ 20 g/kg** seed into the slurry.
   - Coat seeds gently to avoid seed-coat cracking, dry under shade for 2 hours, and sow immediately into moist soil.
   - *Agronomic Impact*: Dual inoculation fixes 30–40 kg of atmospheric Nitrogen per hectare and solubilizes native locked soil phosphorus, saving 50 kg of DAP per hectare.

## 4. Land Configuration & Soil Moisture Conservation in Rainfed Systems

1. **Broad Bed and Furrow (BBF) Planting**:
   - Create 120 cm wide raised beds separated by 30 cm wide, 15 cm deep furrows using a tractor-drawn BBF planter.
   - Sow 4 rows of chickpea or 5 rows of lentil on top of each bed.
   - *Impact*: In case of unseasonal heavy rain, furrows act as safe drainage channels preventing collar root rot; during drought, furrows concentrate light rains into bed root zones.
2. **Dust Mulching & Shallow Interculture**:
   - In cracking *Mar/Kabar* soils, run a hand-hoe or wheel hoe at 25–30 DAS. This breaks soil crust capillaries, forming a 2 cm loose dry surface soil mulch (*dust mulch*) that prevents deep evaporation cracks from drying the root zone.

## 5. Nutrient Management: The Balanced Basal Prescription

Legumes fix their own nitrogen after 20–25 days via root nodules, but require a starter dose and high phosphorus for ATP energy transfer:

$$\text{Recommended Basal Fertilizer Dose} = 20\text{ kg } N + 50\text{ kg } P_2O_5 + 20\text{ kg } K_2O + 20\text{ kg } S\text{ per hectare}$$

| Commercial Fertilizer Source | Quantity per Hectare | Quantity per Acre | Timing & Placement Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Single Super Phosphate (SSP / 16% P2O5, 11% S)** | **312 kg (6.2 bags)** | **125 kg (2.5 bags)** | **Mandatory Basal Band Placement**: Drill 4–5 cm below seed depth. Supplies full phosphorus and 35 kg essential sulphur. |
| **Urea (46% N)** | **43 kg (1 bag)** | **17 kg** | Basal application at sowing as starter nitrogen. Avoid top-dressing urea (triggers vegetative lodging and halts podding). |
| **Muriate of Potash (MOP / 60% K2O)** | **33 kg** | **13 kg** | Basal band placement. Strengthens drought resistance. |

> [!NOTE]
> In acidic red soils (*Rakar/Parwa*), apply **Ammonium Molybdate @ 1.0 g/kg seed** during seed treatment. Molybdenum is the vital catalytic cofactor of nitrogenase enzyme in Rhizobium root nodules.

## 6. Irrigation Scheduling: The Critical Pre-Flowering Rule

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE GOLDEN PULSE WATER RULE**:
> - **NEVER IRRIGATE CHICKPEA DURING ACTIVE FLOWERING (*फूल आते समय सिंचाई कभी न करें*)**.
> - Applying flood irrigation at flowering triggers massive flower drop, stimulates excessive vegetative stem elongation, and induces lethal *Fusarium* wilt and collar rot.

### Recommended Water Windows (If Irrigation is Available):
1. **First Irrigation (Vegetative Stage - 35–45 DAS)**: Apply only if soil moisture is critically depleted before flower bud emergence.
2. **Second Irrigation (Pod Filling Stage - 70–80 DAS)**: Apply light irrigation to prevent premature grain shriveling during sudden March temperature spikes.
3. **Foliar Anti-Stress Spray (For 100% Rainfed Conditions)**:
   - If zero irrigation is available, spray **2.0% Urea** (4 kg in 200 L water/acre) OR **Potassium Nitrate (13:0:45) @ 1.0%** (2 kg in 200 L water/acre) at 50% podding to alleviate terminal drought.

## 7. Integrated Management of Fusarium Wilt & Gram Pod Borer

### A. Fusarium Vascular Wilt (*उकठा रोग / Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. ciceris*)
- **Symptoms**: Sudden wilting and drooping of leaves within 2–3 days without preliminary yellowing; characteristic **dark brown to black vertical vascular discoloration** inside the split taproot.
- **Management Protocol**:
  - Deep summer plowing in May–June to expose chlamydospores to intense solar radiation (>45°C).
  - Crop rotation: Rotate with sorghum, wheat, or mustard; avoid chickpea after chickpea.
  - Biological furrow application: Mix **Trichoderma harzianum @ 2.5 kg enriched in 100 kg well-decomposed FYM/vermicompost per acre** and broadcast into furrow during land preparation.

### B. Gram Pod Borer (*फली छेदक / Helicoverpa armigera*)
- **Damage**: Young greenish larvae feed on leaves, then bore circular holes into developing pods, thrusting their anterior body inside while consuming seeds.
- **Economic Threshold Level (ETL)**: **1–2 larvae per meter row** or 5% damaged pods.
- **Non-Chemical & Biological Tactics**:
  - Install **T-shaped bird perches (*T-खूंटियां*) @ 15–20 per acre** to attract insectivorous birds (Black Drongo, Myna).
  - Install **Helilure Pheromone Traps @ 4–5 per acre** for pest population monitoring.
  - Spray **HaNPV (Helicoverpa armigera Nuclear Polyhedrosis Virus) @ 250 LE/acre** in the late afternoon.

## 8. Farm Safety & Regulatory Notice

> [!CAUTION]
> **MANDATORY SAFETY GATE**: Chemical recommendation requires current label verification with the Central Insecticides Board & Registration Committee (CIBRC) and local agricultural extension authorities before application.
> 
> When pod borer larvae cross ETL (1–2 larvae/meter row) despite biological measures:
> - **Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC** @ **60 ml/acre** (0.3 ml/L water) OR **Emamectin Benzoate 5% SG** @ **80 g/acre** (0.4 g/L water) in 150–200 litres water.
> - Observe mandatory Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI) of at least 14 days.

## 9. Post-Harvest Safe Storage & Pulse Beetle (*Callosobruchus*) Control

1. **Sun Drying**: Dry harvested chickpea and lentil grains for 3–4 days until moisture drops strictly **below 9.0% to 10.0%**.
2. **Edible Oil Seed Coating**: For household and farm seed storage, coat whole grains with **Mustard Oil or Castor Oil @ 5 ml per kg seed** (500 ml/quintal). The thin oil film blocks oxygen exchange through beetle eggs, completely suppressing pulse beetle multiplication without impairing germination or cooking taste.
3. **Hermetic Storage**: Seal dried grains in triple-layer **PICS bags** to eliminate insect pests without synthetic chemicals.
