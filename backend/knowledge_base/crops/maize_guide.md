# Maize Crop Production Guide (मक्के की वैज्ञानिक खेती)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Maize (*मक्का*) is the "Queen of Cereals" with exceptionally high yield potential (24–32 quintals/acre in Kharif; up to 35–40 q/acre in Rabi/Spring under irrigation). Achieving top yields requires well-drained loam soil, sowing on ridges to avoid waterlogging (which destroys root aeration within 24 hours), 3-split Nitrogen application coinciding with active vegetative and flowering stages, basal Zinc Sulphate application to prevent White Bud, and strict prophylactic monitoring against Fall Armyworm (FAW) from the 2-leaf stage onwards.

## When this guidance applies
- **Agro-climatic Zones**: All agricultural zones of Uttar Pradesh (Eastern UP, Central UP, Western UP, and Bundelkhand).
- **Seasons**:
  - *Kharif Maize*: Sowing with pre-monsoon showers (June 15 – July 15).
  - *Rabi Maize*: Sowing in October–November (very high yield potential in UP due to longer grain-filling sunshine).
  - *Spring Maize*: Sowing in late January to February (harvested before peak monsoon).
- **Soil Conditions**: Deep, fertile sandy-loam or loam soils with excellent surface and internal drainage; pH 6.0 to 7.5. Avoid low-lying fields prone to water stagnation.

## Seed Rate, Spacing & Seed Treatment
- **Seed Rate**:
  - Grain Hybrids: 20 kg/ha (8 kg/acre).
  - Sweet Corn: 8–10 kg/ha (3.5–4 kg/acre).
  - Fodder Maize: 40–50 kg/ha (16–20 kg/acre).
- **Seed Treatment (Crucial against Seedling Pests & Blight)**:
  - *Fungicide*: Treat seed with Thiram 75% WP or Captan 50% WP @ 2.5 g/kg seed to prevent seedling rots.
  - *Insecticide (FAW Protection)*: Treat seed with Cyantraniliprole 19.8% + Thiamethoxam 19.8% FS @ 6.0 ml/kg seed. This protects the young seedling against Fall Armyworm and stem borer for the first 15–20 days.
  - *Bio-inoculant*: Inoculate with *Azotobacter* and PSB culture @ 20 g/kg seed.
- **Sowing Method & Spacing**:
  - **Ridge and Furrow Method**: Sow on the side of ridges (two-thirds height from base). This protects roots from water stagnation during heavy rains and facilitates uniform furrow irrigation.
  - Spacing: 60 cm between rows and 20 cm between plants (maintaining a plant population of approx. 83,000 plants/ha or 33,000 plants/acre).
  - Depth of sowing: 4–5 cm in moist soil.

## Balanced Nutrient Management (NPK + Zinc)
For hybrid maize under irrigated conditions, the recommended dose is **120 kg N : 60 kg P2O5 : 50 kg K2O per hectare** (approx. 48:24:20 kg/acre).

### Fertilizer Splitting & Schedule
1. **Basal Application (at sowing)**:
   - Full dose of Phosphorus: DAP @ 130 kg/ha (52 kg/acre) or SSP @ 375 kg/ha (150 kg/acre).
   - Full dose of Potash: MOP @ 83 kg/ha (33 kg/acre). (Essential for strong stalks and drought resilience).
   - 20% of Nitrogen: Urea @ 40 kg/ha (16 kg/acre). (Adjust for DAP nitrogen).
   - **Zinc Sulphate (21% Zn)** @ 25 kg/ha (10 kg/acre): Zinc is critical for maize. Deficiency causes **White Bud** (chlorotic bleaching of newly emerged top leaves).
2. **First Top Dressing (Knee-High Stage, 30–35 DAS)**:
   - Apply 50% of Nitrogen: Neem-coated Urea @ 110 kg/ha (44 kg/acre) side-dressed 8–10 cm away from plant rows, followed by earthing up (*mitti chadhana*).
3. **Second Top Dressing (Tasseling / Silking Stage, 50–55 DAS)**:
   - Apply remaining 30% of Nitrogen: Neem-coated Urea @ 65 kg/ha (26 kg/acre) just before tassel emergence.

## Water Management & Critical Growth Stages
Maize requires 500–600 mm water across its life cycle. It is sensitive to both water deficit and water stagnation:
1. **Knee-High Stage (30–35 DAS)**: Supports rapid stem elongation and leaf formation.
2. **Tasseling Stage (50–55 DAS)**: Emergence of the male flower tassel.
3. **Silking Stage (55–65 DAS)**: **MOST CRITICAL STAGE**. The female silks emerge to catch pollen. Water stress here desynchronizes pollen shedding and silking, causing poorly filled or completely barren cobs.
4. **Milking / Grain Filling Stage (70–80 DAS)**: Requires adequate soil moisture for heavy, plump kernels.
- **Drainage Warning**: If monsoon rains flood the field, drain standing water within 12–24 hours. Prolonged waterlogging turns plants pale yellow and causes permanent root decay.

## Major Pests & Integrated Pest Management (IPM)
- **Fall Armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda)**:
  - *Symptoms*: Pinholes on young leaves, window-paning, ragged leaf margins, and large quantities of sawdust-like brown frass inside the central leaf whorl.
  - *IPM Control*: Scout field weekly. For early whorl infestation, drop a mixture of dry sand + neem cake (9:1 ratio) @ 3–5 g per plant directly into the central whorl.
  - *Chemical Control*: If >10% plants are infested at whorl stage, apply Emamectin Benzoate 5% SG @ 0.4 g/litre or Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC @ 0.4 ml/litre directed into the leaf whorls using a knapsack sprayer without nozzle swirl. See `pests/maize_fall_armyworm.md`.

## Farmer Decision Table
| Field Observation | Likely Cause | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| Young top leaves emerge bleached ivory-white (*White Bud*) | Zinc deficiency | Spray 0.5% Zinc Sulphate (21%) + 0.25% Lime solution. |
| Pinholes on leaves with coarse brown sawdust frass inside whorl | Fall Armyworm (FAW) larva | Apply sand-neem mixture into whorls; spray Emamectin Benzoate if ETL exceeded. |
| Crop leaves yellowing uniformly following heavy continuous rains | Waterlogging / root hypoxia | Drain standing water immediately. Apply foliar spray of 1–2% Urea to revive roots. |
| Poor cob filling; scattered grains on central cob | Moisture stress at silking | Ensure field is well-watered during the 2-week window surrounding silking. |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Makka me fall armyworm se bachav ke liye sabse pehla kadam kya hai? (First step against FAW in maize?)**  
  *A*: Seed treatment with Cyantraniliprole + Thiamethoxam protects the crop for the first 20 days. At 25–30 days, scout the field and drop dry sand mixed with neem cake into the leaf whorls before larvae bore deep into the plant.
- **Q: Kya makka me barish ka pani bhara rehne se nuksan hota hai? (Does waterlogging harm maize?)**  
  *A*: Yes. Maize cannot tolerate standing water. If water stands for over 24 hours, root respiration stops, leaves turn yellow, and yield drops by 30–50%. Always sow on ridges and ensure clear drainage channels.
