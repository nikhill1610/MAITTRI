# Micronutrients Management: Zinc, Iron & Sulphur Comprehensive Guide (सूक्ष्म पोषक तत्व प्रबंधन: जिंक, लोहा एवं गंधक)

## Farmer-friendly summary
While Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Potassium form the primary food of crops, **Micronutrients—especially Zinc ($Zn$), Iron ($Fe$), and Secondary Sulphur ($S$)**—act as the vital catalytic enzymes of plant life. In Uttar Pradesh and the Indo-Gangetic plains, over **45% of cultivated soils are severely deficient in Zinc**, directly causing the infamous **"Khaira Disease" (*खैरा रोग*)** in paddy and **"White Bud" (*सफेद कली*)** in maize. Furthermore, in high-pH calcareous soils, **Iron Chlorosis (*लोहे की कमी से पत्तियों का सफेद होना*)** paralyses young leaves. Rectifying these micro-deficiencies transforms stunted, discolored crops into high-yielding fields, provided farmers master two fundamental rules: **the mandatory addition of slaked lime (*चूना*) when spraying Zinc Sulphate**, and **the absolute prohibition against mixing Zinc with DAP**.

---

## 1. Zinc (Zn) Management: Khaira Disease & White Bud

### Visual Symptoms & Diagnostics
1. **Khaira Disease in Rice (*धान का खैरा रोग*)**:
   - First identified in 1965 by legendary Indian pathologist Dr. Y.L. Nene at Pantnagar.
   - Appears 15 to 25 days after transplanting on the **3rd and 4th leaves from the top**.
   - Chlorotic yellowish patches develop between the veins, rapidly turning into **rusty brown, bronze, or reddish-brown pigmentation** (*तांबे जैसा रंग*).
   - The leaf tissue becomes brittle; plant growth halts; roots turn brown to black with minimal root hair development.
2. **White Bud in Maize (*मक्के की सफेद कली*)**:
   - Newly emerging leaves in the central whorl emerge completely **bleached, pale yellow to ivory-white**.
   - Internodes are drastically shortened, producing dwarfed plants with poor cob development.

### Chemical Incompatibility Rule: Zinc + DAP Prohibition
> [!CAUTION]
> **NEVER MIX ZINC SULPHATE AND DAP IN THE SAME SPRAY TANK OR DRILL**:
> When Zinc Sulphate is mixed with Di-Ammonium Phosphate (DAP), a violent chemical precipitation reaction occurs:
> $$\text{Zinc Sulphate} + \text{DAP} \longrightarrow \mathbf{\text{Zinc Phosphate (Insoluble Solid)}} + \text{Ammonium Sulphate}$$
> Zinc Phosphate forms a chalky white precipitate that completely locks up **BOTH** the Zinc and the Phosphorus into an insoluble solid that plant roots cannot absorb. Furthermore, the thick precipitate clogs sprayer nozzles. Always apply Zinc and DAP **separately**.

### Recommended Application Protocols for Zinc
1. **Soil Application (Best Prophylactic Approach)**:
   - **Zinc Sulphate Heptahydrate (21% Zn)**: Broadcast **25 kg per hectare (10 kg per acre)** as basal during final land preparation once every 2–3 crop seasons.
   - **Zinc Sulphate Monohydrate (33% Zn)**: Broadcast **15 kg per hectare (6 kg per acre)** as basal.
   - Broadcast into the soil separately from phosphate fertilizers.
2. **Foliar Spray Protocol with Lime Neutralization (Emergency Standing Crop Rescue)**:
   - *The Danger of Acid Scorch*: Dissolved Zinc Sulphate produces an acidic solution ($pH \approx 4.5$) that will scorch and burn delicate crop leaves if sprayed alone.
   - **The Mandatory Neutralized Recipe (for 1 Acre / 200 Litres Water)**:
     - Dissolve **1.0 kg Zinc Sulphate (21%)** in 100 litres of clean water.
     - Dissolve **500 grams of Slaked Lime (*बुझा हुआ चूना - Calcium Hydroxide*)** in a separate bucket of 100 litres of water and allow the white sediment to settle.
     - Strain the clear lime water (*चूने का निथरा हुआ पानी*) and mix it with the Zinc Sulphate solution to achieve a neutral $pH \approx 7.0$.
     - Spray thoroughly onto foliage on a calm afternoon. A second spray may be given 10–12 days later.
   - *Chelated Alternative*: If slaked lime is unavailable, spray **Chelated Zinc (Zn-EDTA 12%)** @ 1.0 g per litre of water (200 g/acre). Chelated zinc requires no lime buffer.

## 2. Iron (Fe) Deficiency & Management: Iron Chlorosis

### Visual Symptoms & Diagnostics
- Iron is completely immobile inside the plant; therefore, symptoms appear **strictly on the youngest newly emerging top leaves**.
- Characterized by striking **Interveinal Chlorosis (*नस-हरित लवणता*)**:
  - The leaf blade between the veins turns pale green, then bright lemon-yellow, and finally bleaches into **ivory-white (*दूधिया सफेद रंग*)**.
  - Crucial identification marker: The fine network of veins remains **sharp, dark green** against the bleached white blade.
- **Predisposition**: Calcareous soils with high free calcium carbonate ($CaCO_3$), sodic soils with high pH (>7.8), and sandy soils following heavy upland irrigation.

### Recommended Iron Control Protocol
1. **Foliar Spray (First Line of Relief)**:
   - Soil application of iron is largely ineffective in high-pH calcareous soils because $Fe^{2+}$ rapidly oxidizes into insoluble ferric oxide.
   - **Foliar Recipe**: Dissolve **Ferrous Sulphate (19% Fe / *हरा कसीस*) @ 1.0%** (1.0 kg in 100 litres of water per acre) + **100 g Citric Acid (*नींबू का सत्त*)** or 200 g Urea.
   - Citric acid maintains iron in the soluble ferrous state, allowing rapid foliar absorption. Leaves turn healthy dark green within 5 to 7 days.
2. **Chelated Iron**: Spray **Fe-EDTA (12% Fe)** @ 1.0 g per litre of water.

## 3. Sulphur (S) Deficiency: The Essential Oil & Protein Mineral

### Visual Symptoms & Diagnostics
- Often mistaken for Nitrogen deficiency.
- **The Critical Discriminator**:
  - In Nitrogen deficiency, yellowing starts on the **oldest bottom leaves**.
  - In Sulphur deficiency, uniform pale yellowing appears **first on the youngest upper leaves**, while the bottom leaves stay dark green.
- Leaves become narrow, stems turn hard, thin, and woody, and seed oil content drops significantly in mustard, soybean, and groundnut.

### Recommended Sulphur Control Protocol
1. **Agricultural Gypsum (contains 18% S & 22% Ca)**: Broadcast 150 to 200 kg/ha (60 to 80 kg/acre) as basal at field preparation.
2. **Bentonite Sulphur Granules (90% S)**: Apply 25 kg/ha (10 kg/acre) at sowing.
3. **Single Super Phosphate (SSP)**: Using SSP (11% S) instead of DAP automatically satisfies the entire sulphur requirement for pulses and oilseeds.

## Farmer Quick Decision Table
| Field Observation | Probable Micronutrient Deficit | Emergency Corrective Action |
|---|---|---|
| Paddy leaves showing rusty bronze spots 20 DAT (*Khaira*) | Acute Zinc ($Zn$) Deficiency | Spray 0.5% Zinc Sulphate (21%) neutralized with 0.25% Lime (1 kg ZnSO4 + 500 g Lime in 200 L water/acre). |
| Newly emerging maize top leaves bleached pure white | Maize White Bud ($Zn$ Deficiency) | Spray 0.5% Zinc Sulphate + 0.25% Lime; or spray Zn-EDTA (12%) @ 1 g/L. |
| Youngest top leaves turn ivory-white while veins stay dark green | Iron Chlorosis ($Fe$ Deficiency) | Spray 1.0% Ferrous Sulphate (1 kg/100 L water) + 100 g Citric acid per acre. |
| Upper young leaves turn pale yellow; lower leaves stay green in mustard | Sulphur ($S$) Deficiency | Apply 25 kg/ha Bentonite Sulphur, or spray 0.5% Wettable Sulphur (80% WDG). |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Dhaan me zinc sulphate ke sath chuna kyu milaya jata hai? (Why mix lime with zinc sulphate in rice?)**  
  *A*: Zinc Sulphate dissolved in water is acidic. If sprayed directly without lime, the acidic droplets burn and scorch the green leaf tissue (*पत्ती जल जाती है*). Adding slaked lime (*बुझा हुआ चूना*) neutralizes the acid, ensuring safe and effective leaf absorption.
- **Q: Kya zinc sulphate aur DAP ko ek sath milakar khet me daal sakte hain? (Can Zinc Sulphate and DAP be mixed together?)**  
  *A*: NEVER. Zinc and DAP react instantly to form insoluble Zinc Phosphate. Both nutrients turn into a rock-like solid that neither the plant roots can absorb nor water can dissolve. Always apply Zinc and DAP separately.

## Farm Safety & Handling Precautions
- When slaking quicklime (*कली चूना*), water boils vigorously; wear protective goggles and handle carefully.
- Store Ferrous Sulphate and Zinc Sulphate in sealed airtight bags to prevent moisture absorption and caking.
