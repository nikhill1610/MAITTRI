# Potassium (K) Deficiency & Potash Application Management (पोटैशियम की कमी एवं पोटाश प्रबंधन)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Potassium (*पोटाश / पोटैशियम*) is often termed the "Quality Nutrient" and "Plant Armor." While Nitrogen drives green foliage and Phosphorus builds roots, Potassium regulates the plant's internal water balance, controls the opening and closing of stomata, thickens stem cell walls to **prevent lodging (*फसल का गिरना*)**, and orchestrates the movement of sugars and starches into grain, tubers, and fruits. Crops deficient in Potash exhibit a signature **burnt or scorched appearance along the leaf edges (*किनारों का जलना / Marginal Scorch*)** on older leaves and collapse flat during winds or irrigation. Understanding when to use standard Muriate of Potash (MOP) versus chloride-free Sulphate of Potash (SOP) is critical for potato, vegetable, and pulse growers.

## When this guidance applies
- **Target Crops**: Wheat, Rice, Potato, Maize, Sugarcane, Tomato, and Mustard.
- **Critical Timing**: Basal application at sowing/planting; for long-duration tuber crops (Potato), split application at earthing up.
- **Vulnerable Soils**: Light sandy soils, heavily cropped intensive rice-wheat rotations where farmers historically applied only Urea and DAP, ignoring Potash.

## Visual Identification & Diagnostic Symptoms
1. **Marginal Scorch / Firing (*पत्तियों के किनारों का झुलसना*)**:
   - Because Potassium is highly mobile inside the plant, symptoms strictly appear **first on the oldest lower leaves**.
   - Marginal chlorosis begins at the tip and runs down the outer edges of the leaf.
   - The yellow margins quickly turn dry, necrotic, and dark brown, giving the leaf a distinct **"scorched" or "burnt" look as if touched by fire**, while the central midrib area remains green.
2. **Stem Weakness & Lodging Vulnerability**:
   - Stems develop thin, fragile cell walls in the sclerenchyma tissue.
   - When grain begins filling in wheat or rice, the heavy panicle weight causes stems to bend and lodge flat onto the ground during irrigation or mild winds, causing up to 40% harvesting and grain rot losses.
3. **Impaired Quality & Shelf-Life**:
   - Potato tubers remain small, water-soaked, and bruise easily during transport.
   - Grains are shriveled, light, and low in starch content.

### Lookalikes Triage: Potash Deficiency vs. Salt Burn vs. Moisture Stress
| Feature | Potassium Deficiency | Soil Salinity Burn (*खारा पानी / लवणता*) | Severe Moisture Heat Scorch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Affected Leaves** | **Oldest bottom leaves** strictly | Uniform across both old and young leaves | Exposed upper sun-facing canopy |
| **Visual Pattern** | Burnt dry margins with **healthy green center** | Whitish-brown scorching starting from tip | Whole leaf rolls inwards and wilts uniformly |
| **Soil Correlation**| Low soil test K; sandy textures | High EC (>4 dS/m) or saline tube-well water | Dry parched soil during hot dry winds |

## Arithmetic Fidelity: Elemental $K_2O$ vs. Commercial Potash Products

> [!CAUTION]
> **CONVERSION RULES**:
> Standard agronomic recommendations are published as **$K_2O$ (Potassium Oxide)**, not product weight.
> 
> - **Muriate of Potash (MOP / Potassium Chloride - 0:0:60)** contains **60% K2O**.
>   $$\text{MOP Required (kg)} = \frac{\text{Recommended } K_2O \text{ dose (kg)}}{0.60}$$
>   - For a standard **40 kg K2O/ha** recommendation:
>     $$40 \div 0.60 = \mathbf{66.7\text{ kg MOP per ha}} \quad (\mathbf{26.7\text{ kg MOP per acre}})$$
>   - For a potato **100 kg K2O/ha** recommendation:
>     $$100 \div 0.60 = \mathbf{166.7\text{ kg MOP per ha}} \quad (\mathbf{66.7\text{ kg MOP per acre}})$$
> 
> - **Sulphate of Potash (SOP / Potassium Sulphate - 0:0:50 + 17.5% S)** contains **50% K2O**.
>   $$\text{SOP Required (kg)} = \frac{\text{Recommended } K_2O \text{ dose (kg)}}{0.50}$$
>   - For a **100 kg K2O/ha** dose in potato:
>     $$100 \div 0.50 = \mathbf{200\text{ kg SOP per ha}} \quad (\mathbf{80\text{ kg SOP per acre}})$$

## MOP vs. SOP: The Chloride Sensitivity Principle
- **Muriate of Potash (MOP / 0:0:60)**:
  - Consists of Potassium Chloride ($KCl$). It is the most economical and widely available potash source in India.
  - Excellent for wheat, rice, maize, and mustard.
- **Sulphate of Potash (SOP / 0:0:50)**:
  - Consists of Potassium Sulphate ($K_2SO_4$) and is 100% chloride-free.
  - **The Chloride Rule for Potato & Vegetables**:
    - High concentrations of chloride ($Cl^-$) from MOP cause potatoes to absorb excess water, reducing specific gravity, lowering dry matter, and causing chips to turn soggy.
    - For **seed potato production, processing potato varieties (Kufri Chipsona)**, and chlorine-sensitive crops (Tobacco), **SOP is agronomically superior to MOP**. SOP also supplies 17.5% essential Sulphur.

## Correct Application Timing & Split Methods
- **Basal Placement**: For cereals (Wheat, Rice), apply the entire Potash dose at sowing/transplanting mixed into the soil along with Phosphorus. Potassium binds to clay particles and does not leach as rapidly as nitrogen.
- **Split Doses for Heavy Feeders (Potato & Sugarcane)**:
  - Apply 50% Potash as basal at planting.
  - Apply remaining 50% Potash at earthing up (30–35 DAP) along with nitrogen to directly feed rapid tuber bulking.
- **Emergency Foliar Application for Standing Crops**:
  - If marginal scorch is observed in standing crops, spray **Potassium Nitrate (13:0:45)** @ 1.0% (10 g/L or 1.5 kg/acre in 150 L water) or **SOP (0:0:50)** @ 1.0% solution. Foliar potassium provides rapid relief and builds heat/drought resistance within 5 days.

| Field Condition | Visual Symptom | Action to Take |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Kya gehu aur dhaan me sirf urea aur DAP dalna kafi hai? (Is applying only Urea and DAP enough for wheat and rice?)**  
  *A*: NO. Applying only Urea and DAP starves the crop of Potassium. Without potash, plants develop weak, hollow stems that collapse and fall over (lodge) during wind or irrigation at grain filling, causing up to 30% grain loss. Always apply 25–30 kg MOP per acre.
- **Q: Aalu ke kisan MOP ki jagah SOP kyu pasand karte hain? (Why do potato growers prefer SOP over MOP?)**  
  *A*: MOP contains chloride, which makes potatoes watery and lowers dry matter. SOP is chloride-free, producing firm, dense tubers with higher starch that store much longer in cold storage without rotting and make crisp potato chips.

## Farm Safety & Handling Precautions
- Keep MOP and SOP bags in dry sheds; while less hygroscopic than urea, dampness will cause granules to agglomerate.
- Fertilizer grade potassium is safe to handle; wash skin with fresh water after handling.
