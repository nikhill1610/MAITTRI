# Phosphorus (P) Deficiency Diagnosis & DAP/SSP Placement Guide (फॉस्फोरस की कमी एवं डीएपी / एसएसपी प्रबंधन)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Phosphorus (*फॉस्फोरस / भास्वर*) is the master nutrient responsible for **deep root elongation, vigorous early seedling anchorage, tillering, and healthy seed/grain development**. When phosphorus is deficient, crops exhibit a distinctive **purplish-bronze or reddish discoloration on older leaves** accompanied by severely stunted, shallow root systems. Because Phosphorus is chemically immobile in the soil (moving only 2 to 3 millimeters by diffusion), **it must strictly be placed 4 to 5 cm below and beside the seed at the time of sowing**. Surface broadcasting of DAP onto dry soil is an agronomic failure; understanding the proper choice between Di-Ammonium Phosphate (DAP) and Single Super Phosphate (SSP) is vital for farm profitability.

## When this guidance applies
- **Applies to**: All agronomic, pulse, and oilseed crops (Wheat, Chickpea, Mustard, Rice, Maize, Potato).
- **Critical Application Window**: Strictly at **sowing or transplanting (Basal Application)**. Top dressing phosphorus onto standing crops is completely ineffective.
- **Problem Soils**: Acidic soils (pH <6.0) where phosphorus gets fixed by Iron and Aluminum, and alkaline/calcareous soils (pH >8.0) where phosphorus precipitates with Calcium.

## Visual Identification & Diagnostic Discrimination
1. **Purplish-Bronze Pigmentation (*बैंगनी या गहरा कांस्य रंग*)**:
   - Because phosphorus is mobile within the plant, older lower leaves show symptoms first.
   - Leaves turn dark bluish-green initially, followed by the accumulation of anthocyanin pigments, creating a distinct **purplish, reddish, or bronzed tint along the leaf margins, petioles, and underside of veins**.
2. **Severely Stunted Root System**:
   - Tap roots and secondary feeder roots fail to elongate.
   - Plants remain dwarfed, with thin, stiff, spindly stems and reduced tillering.
3. **Delayed Flowering & Chaffy Grain**:
   - Crop maturity is delayed by 10 to 14 days; seed set is poor, producing light, poorly filled grains.

### Lookalikes Triage: Phosphorus Deficiency vs. Winter Cold Shock
| Parameter | Phosphorus (P) Deficiency | Winter Cold Injury (*सर्दियों में ठंड का असर*) |
|---|---|---|
| **Affected Leaves** | Strictly **older lower leaves** first | Exposed **younger top leaves** show purple tips |
| **Root Growth** | Extremely stunted, few lateral roots | Roots healthy, white, and well-branched |
| **Recovery** | Does not recover without P fertilization | Recovers immediately as sunshine warms up the field |

## The Golden Agronomic Law: Sub-Surface Placement Mechanics

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **WHY SURFACE BROADCASTING OF DAP FAILS**:
> Unlike Nitrogen (which dissolves and moves readily with irrigation water), **Phosphorus does NOT move with water**. It binds tightly to soil clay minerals within 2 to 3 mm of the fertilizer granule.
> 
> - **The 4–5 cm Deep Band Rule**:
>   DAP or SSP must be placed **4 to 5 cm below the seed and 3 to 4 cm to the side** using a seed-cum-fertilizer drill (*नाई युक्त सीड ड्रिल*).
> - When the seedling germinates and its first primary radicle root grows downwards, it strikes the concentrated phosphorus band right at the crown root zone, absorbing 100% of the nutrient.
> - Broadcasting DAP on the soil surface leaves the phosphorus trapped in the dry top 1 cm crust where plant roots never venture, wasting 60% to 80% of the fertilizer money.

## Arithmetic & Product Selection: DAP vs. SSP

| Parameter | Di-Ammonium Phosphate (DAP / 18:46:0) | Single Super Phosphate (SSP / 0:16:0) |
|---|---|---|
| **Nutrient Content** | **46% P2O5** + **18% Nitrogen** | **16% P2O5** + **11–12% Sulphur** + **19–21% Calcium** |
| **Best Suited Crops** | Cereal crops (Wheat, Paddy, Maize) | **Oilseeds (Mustard) and Pulses (Gram, Lentil)** |
| **Sulphur Supply** | Zero (0% Sulphur) | Supplies 11 kg pure Sulphur with every 100 kg bag! |
| **Cost per Bag** | Higher initial cost | Lower cost per bag; delivers 3 essential nutrients |

### Calculation Formulas
1. **To supply 60 kg P2O5/ha using DAP (46% P2O5)**:
   $$\text{DAP Required} = \frac{60}{0.46} = \mathbf{130.4\text{ kg DAP per ha}} \quad (\mathbf{52.2\text{ kg DAP per acre}})$$
   *(Note: 130 kg DAP also supplies $130.4 \times 0.18 = 23.5\text{ kg Nitrogen}$. Subtract this from your basal urea dose).*
2. **To supply 40 kg P2O5/ha using SSP (16% P2O5)**:
   $$\text{SSP Required} = \frac{40}{0.16} = \mathbf{250\text{ kg SSP per ha}} \quad (\mathbf{100\text{ kg SSP per acre}})$$
   *(Bonus: 250 kg SSP automatically delivers 28 kg of Sulphur, fulfilling the entire oil-synthesis requirement for mustard).*

## Overcoming Soil Fixation: Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB)
In Indian soils, 70% to 80% of applied phosphorus becomes chemically locked ("fixed") into insoluble calcium phosphate or iron phosphate complexes.
- **PSB Biofertilizer Inoculation**:
  - Treat seeds before sowing with **Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB - *Pseudomonas striata* or *Bacillus megaterium*)** culture @ 200 g per 10 kg seed.
  - PSB secretes organic acids (citric, gluconic acids) that dissolve insoluble soil-bound phosphorus, converting it into plant-absorbable orthophosphate ions ($H_2PO_4^-$).
  - Using PSB increases phosphorus recovery by 20% to 25% and saves up to 10 kg P2O5 per acre.

## Farmer Decision Table
| Crop Category | Soil Test / Field Condition | Best Fertilizer Choice & Dose |
|---|---|---|
| Wheat / Maize | Irrigated alluvial plains; medium P status | Apply DAP @ 50–55 kg/acre drilled 4–5 cm below seed at sowing. |
| Mustard (Sarson) | Sandy loam; high oil content desired | Apply SSP @ 100 kg/acre (supplies full P + Sulphur for oil synthesis) as basal. |
| Chickpea / Gram | Rainfed pulses; root nodulation critical | Apply SSP @ 75–80 kg/acre + PSB seed treatment; place basally in furrow. |
| Standing crop (40 days old) showing purple leaf veins | Acute P deficiency in standing crop | Top dressing DAP will fail. Spray foliar 1–2% DAP solution (clean supernatant) or 0.5% 12:61:0 (Mono-Ammonium Phosphate). |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Khadi fasal me DAP chhidakne se fayda kyu nahi hota? (Why does top-dressing DAP on standing crops fail?)**  
  *A*: Phosphorus cannot move downwards through the soil with irrigation water. When broadcast on top of the field, DAP stays locked in the top 1 cm of dry crust. Plant feeder roots are 5 to 15 cm deep and cannot reach it. Always drill DAP at sowing time.
- **Q: Sarson aur daal wali faslo ke liye SSP, DAP se behtar kyu hai? (Why is SSP better than DAP for mustard and pulses?)**  
  *A*: DAP contains only Nitrogen and Phosphorus. Pulses and mustard urgently require **Sulphur** for oil synthesis and protein nodules. Single Super Phosphate (SSP) contains 11–12% Sulphur and 20% Calcium in addition to Phosphorus, making it agronomically superior for oilseeds and pulses.

## Farm Safety & Handling Precautions
- Do not mix DAP directly with Zinc Sulphate in the same hopper; they react instantly to form insoluble Zinc Phosphate, rendering both nutrients unabsorbable.
- Store fertilizer bags in moisture-proof sheds off the bare floor.
