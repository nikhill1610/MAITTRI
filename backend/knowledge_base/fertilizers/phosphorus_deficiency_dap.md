# Phosphorus Deficiency in Crops (फास्फोरस की कमी व डीएपी/एसएसपी प्रबंधन)

## Visual Deficiency Symptoms
Phosphorus (P) is vital for cellular energy transfer (ATP), rapid root proliferation, tillering, and timely grain maturity.
- Characteristic Purple or Bronze Discoloration: Deficient plants show distinct purplish, reddish, or bronzed pigmentation on older lower leaves, leaf sheaths, and stems, especially noticeable along leaf veins in cold weather.
- Stunted Root Development: Poor primary and secondary root system development, resulting in weak anchoring and restricted water/nutrient uptake.
- Delayed Flowering & Maturity: Earhead or pod emergence is delayed, and grain filling is incomplete.
- Thin & Weak Stalks: In maize and wheat, stems remain thin with limited tillering.

## Correction & Management Guidelines
1. Basal Application is Crucial:
   - Phosphorus is immobile in soil; surface top-dressing after crop emergence has very poor efficacy.
   - Entire recommended dose of Phosphorus must be placed in the root zone (band placement 3-5 cm below seed) as a basal application at sowing time.
2. Fertilizer Options:
   - Di-Ammonium Phosphate (DAP 18:46:0): Apply 50 kg/acre at sowing for wheat/paddy.
   - Single Super Phosphate (SSP 0:16:0): High-recommendation alternative (apply 150 kg/acre). SSP contains 16% P, 11% Sulphur, and 19% Calcium, making it especially effective in oilseeds (mustard), pulses, and alkaline soils.
3. Biofertilizers:
   - Inoculate seeds with Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB) @ 10g/kg seed or soil application of PSB culture @ 2 kg/acre mixed in 100 kg moist FYM to solubilize fixed native soil phosphorus.
