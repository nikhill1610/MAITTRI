# Nitrogen Deficiency in Crops (नाइट्रोजन की कमी के लक्षण एवं यूरिया प्रबंधन)

## Visual Identification & Deficiency Symptoms
Nitrogen (N) is a highly mobile macronutrient within the plant system.
- Older Lower Leaves Turn Yellow First: Because the plant translocates nitrogen from mature tissue to rapidly growing upper shoots, deficiency symptoms always initiate on older lower leaves.
- Characteristic "V" Shaped Yellowing: In cereals like Wheat, Maize, and Rice, yellowing begins at the leaf tip and progresses down the midrib in an inverted 'V' pattern, while leaf margins remain green initially.
- General Symptoms:
  - Stunted plant growth, thin spindly stems, reduced tillering in wheat/paddy.
  - Overall pale yellow-green canopy (chlorosis) due to inhibited chlorophyll synthesis.
  - Premature senescence and drop of lower foliage.
- How to Distinguish from Other Causes:
  - Iron Deficiency: Yellowing occurs on the youngest top new leaves, with veins remaining dark green (interveinal chlorosis).
  - Waterlogging: Causes general yellowing due to root suffocation; soil moisture is at saturation.
  - Yellow Rust: Yellow powdery pustules wipe off on a tissue; nitrogen deficiency does not produce powdery dust.

## Remedial Measures & Urea Dosing
1. Split Top-Dressing:
   - For Wheat: Apply Urea @ 45-50 kg/acre at 1st irrigation (CRI stage, 21-25 DAS) and 2nd split before boot stage (45-50 DAS).
   - For Rice: Apply Urea in 3 splits (basal, active tillering, panicle initiation).
   - Always apply Urea when soil is moist, never on completely dry or deeply flooded fields. Incorporate into the soil to prevent volatile ammonia loss.
2. Foliar Emergency Rescue (Nano Urea / 2% Urea Spray):
   - In standing crops with severe yellowing, spray 2% Urea solution (20g Urea per litre of water = 4 kg in 200L water/acre), OR
   - IFFCO Nano Urea @ 4 ml per litre of water (500 ml/acre) at active vegetative tillering and pre-flowering stage for rapid foliar absorption.
