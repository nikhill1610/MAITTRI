# Nitrogen (N) Deficiency Diagnosis & Urea Management Guide (नाइट्रोजन की कमी एवं यूरिया प्रबंधन)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Nitrogen (*नाइट्रोजन*) is the primary engine of vegetative plant growth, dark green leaf foliage, and protein synthesis in agricultural crops. Because Nitrogen is highly mobile within the plant vascular system, deficiency symptoms strictly appear **first on the oldest lower bottom leaves**, forming a characteristic **'V'-shaped yellowing starting from the leaf tip and advancing backwards along the midrib**. Rectifying nitrogen hunger requires understanding the mathematical difference between elemental Nitrogen ($N$) and commercial Urea ($46\% N$), utilizing mandatory slow-release Neem-Coated Urea (NCU) in calibrated split applications, and avoiding excessive late-stage dumping of urea, which triggers plant lodging and fungal epidemics.

## When this guidance applies
- **Applies to**: All major Indian cereal, oilseed, and vegetable crops (Wheat, Rice, Maize, Mustard, Potato, Tomato).
- **Critical Growth Stages**: Active vegetative tillering, branching, and panicle/spikelet initiation.
- **Soil Predisposition**: Sandy soils with low organic matter (<0.5% Organic Carbon), heavily leached soils following monsoon flooding, and waterlogged fields with poor aeration.

## Visual Identification & Diagnostic Discrimination
1. **The Classic 'V'-Shaped Chlorosis Pattern**:
   - Because Nitrogen is readily translocated from older tissues to feed new growing leaves, deficiency symptoms **strictly originate on the lowest, oldest leaves**.
   - Yellowing starts at the **leaf tip** and progresses backwards along the central **midrib in an inverted 'V' shape**, leaving the outer leaf edges green in the early stages.
   - Gradually, the entire lower leaf turns golden-yellow, withers, dries to light brown, and dies prematurely.
2. **Plant Stunting & Canopy Vigor**:
   - Plants exhibit a pale, yellowish-green stunted appearance (*हल्का पीलापन*).
   - Tiller production in wheat and rice is drastically reduced; stems remain thin, spindly, and erect.

### Lookalike Triage: Nitrogen vs. Zinc vs. Iron vs. Sulphur
| Nutrient Deficiency | Earliest Leaf Affected | Visual Chlorosis Pattern | Discriminator |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nitrogen (N)** | **Oldest bottom leaves** | **'V'-shaped chlorosis** starting from tip along midrib | Leaf tips dry up; general yellowing from bottom upwards |
| **Sulphur (S)** | **Youngest top leaves** | Uniform pale yellowing across the entire new leaf | Opposite of Nitrogen: upper leaves turn yellow first, bottom leaves stay green |
| **Zinc (Zn)** | **3rd and 4th leaves from top** | Bleached bronze/rusty spots between veins (*Khaira*) | Midrib remains green; leaf blade turns rusty brown and brittle |
| **Iron (Fe)** | **Youngest emerging top leaves**| Distinct **interveinal chlorosis**; leaf blade bleaches ivory-white | Leaf veins remain sharp dark green like a network |

## Arithmetic Fidelity: Elemental Nitrogen ($N$) vs. Commercial Urea Product

> [!CAUTION]
> **CRITICAL ARONOMIC DISTINCTION**:
> Never confuse **Elemental Nitrogen ($N$)** with **Commercial Fertilizer (Urea)**.
> Commercial Neem-Coated Urea contains **46% Nitrogen**. That means **100 kg of Urea delivers only 46 kg of actual Nitrogen**.
> 
> **Conversion Formula**:
> $$\text{Commercial Urea Required (kg)} = \frac{\text{Elemental Nitrogen Recommended (kg)}}{0.46}$$
> 
> - **Standard 120 kg N/ha Recommendation**:
>   $$120\text{ kg } N \div 0.46 = \mathbf{260.8\text{ kg Urea per hectare}} \quad (\mathbf{104.3\text{ kg Urea per acre}})$$
> - **DAP Correction Factor**:
>   If applying Di-Ammonium Phosphate (DAP, which contains $18\% N$ and $46\% P_2O_5$): A basal application of 100 kg DAP automatically delivers **18 kg of Nitrogen**. You must subtract this 18 kg N from your total urea requirement.

## Calibrated Application Timing: The Split-Dose Principle
**Never apply the entire Urea dose at the time of sowing**. Nitrogen in the form of amide ($NH_2$) and nitrate ($NO_3^-$) is highly prone to:
1. Leaching losses down beyond the root zone with heavy water.
2. Ammonia volatilization losses into the air under hot alkaline soil conditions.

### Standard 3-Split Schedule for Cereal Crops (Wheat & Rice)
1. **Basal Dose (at Sowing / Transplanting)**:
   - Apply **20% to 25% of total Nitrogen** (approx. 20–25 kg Urea/acre). This provides early seedling vigor without risking fertilizer burn.
2. **First Top Dressing (Active Tillering / CRI, 20–25 DAS/DAT)**:
   - Apply **40% to 50% of total Nitrogen** (approx. 40–50 kg Urea/acre) alongside the first irrigation. Crown root and tiller formation peak at this stage.
3. **Second Top Dressing (Panicle Initiation / Jointing, 45–50 DAS/DAT)**:
   - Apply remaining **30% of total Nitrogen** (approx. 30–35 kg Urea/acre).
   - **Cut-Off Warning**: Complete all urea top dressing before booting/heading. Applying urea after heading produces weak straw, delays maturity, increases plant lodging, and invites severe blast and insect attacks.

## Neem-Coated Urea (NCU) & Liquid Nano Urea
1. **Neem-Coated Urea (NCU)**:
   - The oil coating of neem acts as a natural **nitrification inhibitor**, slowing down the bacterial conversion of ammonium into leachable nitrates by 15–20 days.
   - Increases Nitrogen Use Efficiency (NUE) by 10–15% compared to plain urea.
2. **Liquid Nano Urea (IFFCO)**:
   - Formulated as nano-particles (20–50 nm) of nitrogen in liquid suspension (4% N / 40,000 mg/L).
   - *Foliar Application*: Spray 2.0 to 4.0 ml of Nano Urea per litre of water (500 ml in 125–150 L water per acre) at the active tillering/branching stage (30–35 DAS) and pre-flowering stage (50–55 DAS).
   - Directly enters leaf stomata, reducing the need for the second top dressing of conventional granular urea by up to 1 bag (45 kg) per acre.

## Farmer Decision Table
| Field Condition | Visual Symptom | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| Bottom leaves turning pale yellow in 'V' shape on 25-day-old wheat | Acute Nitrogen Starvation | Top dress 35–40 kg Neem-Coated Urea per acre immediately with irrigation; or spray 2% Urea foliar solution. |
| Excessive dark green foliage; soft succulent stems; delayed flowering | Nitrogen Toxicity / Overdosing | Stop all nitrogen applications immediately. Apply Muriate of Potash (MOP @ 20 kg/acre) to strengthen cell walls and prevent lodging. |
| Waterlogged field after heavy rainfall; plants turn pale yellow | Temporary root suffocation and N leaching | Drain standing water. Spray 2% Urea solution (2 kg Urea in 100 L water) directly onto leaves to bypass damaged roots. |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Kya urea ko pani lagane ke pehle dalna chahiye ya baad me? (Should urea be applied before or after irrigation?)**  
  *A*: In loamy and sandy soils, apply urea 1 day **after irrigation** or when the soil surface has dried enough to walk on (*ओट आने पर*). Broadcasting urea into deep standing water causes heavy leaching below the root zone. In heavy clay soils, applying just before a light irrigation is acceptable.
- **Q: 2% Urea ghol (foliar spray) kaise banate hain? (How to prepare a 2% urea foliar spray?)**  
  *A*: Dissolve exactly 2.0 kg of good quality Urea in 100 litres of clean water (or 400 g in a 20-litre knapsack sprayer tank). Spray on foliage during late afternoon. This provides rapid greening within 48–72 hours during acute deficiency.

## Farm Safety & Handling Precautions
- Store Urea bags in a dry, elevated platform away from moisture (urea is highly hygroscopic and cakes into hard rocks).
- Avoid skin contact with dissolved urea fertilizers; wash hands thoroughly after application.
