# Wheat Irrigation Scheduling & Critical CRI Stage Guide (गेहूं में सिंचाई प्रबंधन एवं सीआरआई अवस्था)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Irrigation timing is the single most decisive factor determining wheat grain yield in Uttar Pradesh and the Indo-Gangetic plains. Wheat requires between 400 and 500 mm of water, distributed across **4 to 6 critical physiological growth stages**. Foremost among these is the **Crown Root Initiation (CRI / *ताज मूल अवस्था*) stage at 20 to 25 days after sowing (DAS)**. Moisture stress at the CRI stage permanently halts the emergence of secondary crown roots and tillers, slashing yield by **25% to 35%**. Conversely, applying excessively heavy flood irrigation on heavy soils at CRI causes prolonged water stagnation, depriving young roots of oxygen and turning the crop pale yellow.

## The 6 Critical Physiological Irrigation Stages of Wheat
| Stage # | Growth Stage Name (English / Hindi) | Typical Timing (DAS) | Physiological Significance | Yield Loss if Skipped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Crown Root Initiation (CRI) / ताज मूल** | **20–25 DAS** | **MOST CRITICAL**. Transition from temporary seed roots to permanent crown feeder root system. | **25% – 35%** |
| **2** | **Tillering / कल्ले निकलने की अवस्था** | 40–45 DAS | Supports vigorous lateral shoot development and leaf area index. | 12% – 15% |
| **3** | **Late Jointing / गांठे बनने की अवस्था** | 60–65 DAS | Rapid stem elongation and spikelet number determination. | 10% – 12% |
| **4** | **Flowering / Anthesis / फूल आने की अवस्था** | 80–85 DAS | Pollination and fertilization. Avoid high wind irrigation. | 15% – 20% |
| **5** | **Milking / दूधिया अवस्था** | 100–105 DAS | Starch and protein accumulate into grain in liquid milk state. | 10% – 15% |
| **6** | **Dough / दाना पकने की अवस्था** | 115–120 DAS | Grain hardening; prevents premature desiccation from hot westerly winds. | 5% – 8% |

## What is the CRI Stage and Why is it Supreme?
- **Crown Root Emergence**: The seedling initially germinates using temporary seminal roots fed by the seed kernel. Between 20 and 25 days after sowing, permanent nodal roots (**Crown Roots / *ताज मूल***) emerge approximately 2 to 3 cm beneath the soil surface.
- **The Anchor of Tillers**: Every productive tiller (*कल्ले*) that produces a grain-bearing earhead emerges from this crown root node.
- **Moisture Requirement**: If the top 5 cm of soil is dry when crown roots emerge, their elongation aborts. The plant remains restricted to a single frail main stem with 1–2 poor tillers instead of 5–8 vigorous tillers.
- **Light Irrigation Rule**: The first irrigation at CRI must **always be light (4 to 5 cm depth)**. Flooding the field deeply (7–10 cm) in heavy alluvial clay soils drowns the young crown roots in anoxic (oxygen-deprived) mud, causing severe root hypoxia and widespread yellowing.

## Water Allocation Priority under Limited Water Availability
When tube-well electricity or canal water supply is severely restricted, follow the official ICAR-IIWBR priority matrix:
1. **If ONLY ONE irrigation is available**:
   - Apply strictly at the **CRI Stage (20–25 DAS)**. (Gives highest marginal return on water).
2. **If ONLY TWO irrigations are available**:
   - 1st Irrigation: **CRI Stage (20–25 DAS)**.
   - 2nd Irrigation: **Flowering / Anthesis Stage (80–85 DAS)**.
3. **If ONLY THREE irrigations are available**:
   - 1st Irrigation: **CRI Stage (20–25 DAS)**.
   - 2nd Irrigation: **Late Jointing Stage (60–65 DAS)**.
   - 3rd Irrigation: **Milking Stage (100–105 DAS)**.
4. **If FOUR irrigations are available**:
   - Apply at CRI (21 DAS), Tillering (45 DAS), Flowering (85 DAS), and Milking (105 DAS).

## Weather Precaution: The Wind & Lodging Hazard
- **Heading & Milking Vulnerability (February & March)**:
  - During the flowering and milking stages, wheat plants are top-heavy with grain weight.
  - If field soil is flooded and soft, even moderate wind speeds (**>15 km/h**) will cause the entire crop to buckle and lodge flat into the mud (*फसल गिर जाना*).
  - **The Action Rule**: Always check the local 3-day IMD wind and thunderstorm forecast before scheduling the 4th, 5th, or 6th irrigation. If windy days are forecasted, postpone irrigation until calm, windless conditions return.

## Lookalikes Triage: Why are Wheat Leaves Yellow after First Irrigation?
| Visual Field Observation | Real Cause | Action Required |
|---|---|---|
| Entire field turned pale yellow 3–5 days after first irrigation on heavy clay soil | **Waterlogging / Root Hypoxia**: Over-flooded irrigation filled all soil pores, suffocating crown roots. | Drain standing water from low spots. Spray foliar 2% Urea solution (2 kg in 100 L water/acre) to feed leaves directly while roots recover. |
| Patches of yellow linear stripes with yellow dust on fingers | **Yellow Rust Fungal Infection** | Spray Propiconazole 25% EC (1 ml/L) immediately. Consult `diseases/wheat_yellow_rust.md`. |
| Oldest bottom leaves turn yellow in 'V' shape while new leaves are green | **Normal Nitrogen Starvation** | Top dress 35–40 kg Neem-Coated Urea per acre immediately. |

## Farmer Decision Table
| Crop Age | Soil Moisture State | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| 21–23 days old; soil dry at 3 cm depth | Crown root emergence active | Apply light border irrigation (4–5 cm). Follow with 35 kg/acre Urea top dressing. |
| 45 days old; crop tillering well; no rain in 3 weeks | Active tillering stage | Apply 2nd irrigation; follow with final split of Urea. |
| 85 days old (flowering); strong gusty winds forecasted tomorrow | High lodging risk | **HOLD IRRIGATION**. Wait until winds drop below 12 km/h before watering. |
| 120 days old; grains hard when pressed with fingernail | Crop maturity reached | Stop all irrigation completely. Allow field to dry for combine harvesting. |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Kya gehu me pehli sinchai 21 din se pehle ki ja sakti hai? (Can first irrigation be done before 21 days?)**  
  *A*: Only if the pre-sowing irrigation (*पलेवा*) was inadequate and topsoil is bone dry (causing poor germination). Under normal palewa, crown roots emerge between 20–25 days. Irrigating at 10–12 days is wasteful and chills the soil unnecessarily.
- **Q: Pehli sinchai ke baad gehu peela kyu pad jata hai? (Why does wheat turn yellow after first irrigation?)**  
  *A*: Due to heavy over-flooding. When too much water is applied to heavy loamy or clay soil, water stands in puddles for days. Air cannot reach the young roots, stopping nutrient uptake. Always ensure light irrigation and level field plots.
