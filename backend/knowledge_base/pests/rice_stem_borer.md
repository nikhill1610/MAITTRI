# Yellow Stem Borer in Rice: Integrated Pest Management (धान का पीला तना छेदक कीट)

## Farmer-friendly summary
The Yellow Stem Borer (*Scirpophaga incertulas / धान का पीला तना छेदक*) is the most persistent and damaging monophagous insect pest of rice in Uttar Pradesh and Northern India. Because the moth larva feeds invisibly inside the plant culm (stem), surface damage only becomes obvious after vascular destruction has already occurred. In the vegetative tillering stage, the severed central shoot wilts into a **"Dead Heart" (*डेड हार्ट*)**; in the reproductive heading stage, the damaged tiller produces an empty, erect, bleached panicle termed **"White Earhead" (*सफेद बाली / व्हाइट इयरहेड*)**, resulting in complete grain loss from affected tillers.

## When this guidance applies
- **Agro-climatic Zones**: All paddy-growing districts of Uttar Pradesh.
- **Vulnerable Crop Stages**: Active tillering (30–45 DAT) and panicle initiation/booting to flowering (60–80 DAT).
- **Seasonal Behavior**: Multiple overlapping generations during the Kharif monsoon season (July to October); adult moths emerge in swarms during warm, sultry evenings with high humidity.

## Life Cycle & Diagnostic Symptoms

### 1. Egg Masses & Larval Entry
- Female moths are bright yellow/straw-colored with a conspicuous black spot on each forewing and a tuft of yellow silken hairs on the anal tip.
- Eggs are laid in oval clusters of 50–100 eggs covered with buff-colored felt hairs near the **tips of young nursery and field leaves**.
- Newly hatched tiny larvae crawl down the leaf sheath, bore a pinhole entry into the lower stem just above the waterline, and spend their entire life feeding on the inner vascular marrow.

### 2. Vegetative Phase: "Dead Heart" (*डेड हार्ट*)
- Feeding larva cuts off xylem and phloem transport to the central emerging leaf.
- The central whorl turns pale yellow, wilts, and dries up into a straw-colored dead shoot.
- **The Pull Test**: Gently tug the dried central shoot. It pulls out effortlessly with zero resistance, revealing a chewed, brownish, chewed-off base emitting a slight rotting odor with visible larval frass (*कीड़े का मल*).

### 3. Reproductive Phase: "White Earhead" (*सफेद बाली*)
- Infestation during booting or panicle emergence severs the vascular supply to the flowering rachis.
- The emergent panicle emerges completely bleached white, standing stiffly upright with 100% empty, chaffy spikelets (*फोथरा / पोचा दाना*).

## Lookalike Discrimination: White Earhead vs. Neck Blast
| Feature | Stem Borer White Earhead (*तना छेदक*) | Rice Neck Blast (*गर्दन-तोड़ ब्लास्ट*) |
|---|---|---|
| **Symptom Type** | Insect larval boring | Airborne fungal attack (*Pyricularia*) |
| **Pull Test Result** | Pulls out effortlessly from the stem base with a light finger tug | Does **not** pull out; securely anchored |
| **Stem Neck Base** | Clean panicle neck; damage and frass are at the **bottom base** of the stem | Panicle neck node is **shiny black, rotten, and shriveled** right at the panicle base |

## Recommended Action & IPM Management Protocols

### 1. Cultural & Mechanical Sanitation
- **Seedling Tip Clipping (*पौध के सिरों की कटाई*)**:
  - Before transplanting nursery seedlings into the main field, clip off the top 2 to 3 cm of seedling leaf tips.
  - *Why Mandatory*: Over 85% of stem borer egg masses are deposited on the top 2 cm of seedling leaves. Clipping and burning leaf tips destroys thousands of potential borer larvae before they reach the main field.
- **Pheromone Trapping (Mass Trapping & Monitoring)**:
  - Install Sleeve Pheromone Traps baited with **Scirpolure** @ 8 traps per acre at 20–25 days after transplanting.
  - Position traps 30 cm above the canopy; replace lures every 30 days. Trap catches of >4 moths/trap/day indicate imminent larval hatching.
- **Biocontrol**:
  - Release the egg parasitoid *Trichogramma japonicum* @ 1,00,000 parasitized eggs/ha (approx. 40,000/acre via Tricho-cards) starting at 30 DAT at weekly intervals (3 releases).

### 2. Chemical Control Protocols (CIBRC / University Label Verified)
- **Economic Threshold Level (ETL)**:
  - Vegetative stage: 5% to 10% Dead Hearts.
  - Flowering stage: 1 adult moth/m² or 1 egg mass/m², or >2% White Earheads.
- **Granular Broadcasting (Preferred in standing water, 25–40 DAT)**:
  - Maintain 2 to 3 cm standing water in the field for at least 48 hours after application.
  1. **Cartap hydrochloride 4% G** @ 18 to 20 kg/ha (7.5 to 8.0 kg/acre). *(Absorbed systemically through roots and translocated to the inner stem culm).*
  2. **Chlorantraniliprole 0.4% G (Ferterra)** @ 10 kg/ha (4.0 kg/acre).
  3. **Fipronil 0.3% G** @ 20 to 25 kg/ha (8 to 10 kg/acre).
- **Foliar Sprays (If water cannot be retained or at heading stage)**:
  1. **Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC (Coragen)** @ 0.3 ml/litre of water (60 ml in 200 L water per acre).
  2. **Flubendiamide 39.35% SC (Fame)** @ 0.25 ml/litre of water (50 ml in 200 L water per acre).

## Farmer Decision Table
| Field Observation | Infestation Stage | Action to Take |
|---|---|---|
| Nursery seedlings ready for transplanting | Pre-planting phase | Clip top 2–3 cm leaf tips of seedlings to destroy borer egg masses. |
| Dried central whorls (Dead Hearts) pull out easily in 5–8% hills | Vegetative tillering ETL reached | Broadcast Cartap hydrochloride 4% G (8 kg/acre) in 2–3 cm standing water. |
| Scirpolure pheromone traps catching >5 yellow moths per night | Adult swarming peak | Release Tricho-cards or spray Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC to kill newly hatched larvae before they bore into stems. |
| White erect panicles already visible across field | Heading stage damage | Larvae have already exited or pupated inside; grain loss is irreversible. Note for seed clipping next season. |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Dhaan ki ropai se pehle patti ka siri (tip) katna kyu faydemand hai? (Why clip seedling tips before transplanting?)**  
  *A*: Yellow stem borer moths lay their buff-colored egg masses exclusively near the leaf tips of seedlings. Clipping and destroying the top 2–3 cm of leaf tips destroys these eggs before they hatch, eliminating early stem borer attacks in the main field.
- **Q: Cartap hydrochloride daalte samay pani rakhna kyu zaroori hai? (Why keep standing water when applying Cartap granules?)**  
  *A*: Cartap hydrochloride 4% G is a root-absorbed systemic chemical. It dissolves in standing water (2–3 cm) and is taken up through the rice roots into the inner stem tissue where the borer feeds. Without water, the chemical cannot be absorbed.
