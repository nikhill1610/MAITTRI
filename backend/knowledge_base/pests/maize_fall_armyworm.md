# Fall Armyworm (FAW) in Maize: Integrated Pest Management Guide (मक्के का फॉल आर्मीवर्म)

## Farmer-friendly summary
The Fall Armyworm (*Spodoptera frugiperda / मक्के का फॉल आर्मीवर्म*), an invasive moth native to the Americas that invaded India in 2018, is the most aggressive insect pest attacking maize in Uttar Pradesh. The caterpillars feed voraciously inside the protective funnel-shaped central leaf whorl (*गोफ*), leaving jagged leaf holes, window-paned foliage, and heavy accumulations of sawdust-like brown droppings (*frass*). If uncontrolled during the vegetative stage, FAW bores into developing cobs, causing yield reductions of 30% to over 70%. Successful control relies on scouting within the first 15–20 days, dropping sand-neem mixtures into young whorls, and targeting sprays directly into the leaf funnel before larvae mature into cob borers.

## When this guidance applies
- **Agro-climatic Zones**: All maize-growing districts of Uttar Pradesh across Kharif, Rabi, and Spring seasons.
- **Vulnerable Crop Stages**:
  - *Seedling to Early Whorl (V1–V4, 8–20 DAS)*: Most critical window for timely intervention.
  - *Mid-Whorl to Tasseling (V5–VT, 25–50 DAS)*: High foliage consumption phase.
  - *Cob Development Stage*: Larvae bore under husk into kernel ear tips.

## Diagnostic Identification & Larval Morphology
1. **The Inverted 'Y' on Head Capsule**:
   - Mature larvae (3rd to 6th instar, 30–40 mm long) have a dark, hardened head capsule marked with a prominent, distinct **white/yellow inverted 'Y' suture (*उल्टा Y निशान*)**.
2. **The 4-Spot Square Marker**:
   - Examine the 8th abdominal segment (near the posterior tail end). Four raised dark spots (*pinacula*) are arranged in a **perfect square**, whereas spots on other body segments form a trapezoid.
3. **Feeding Symptoms & Sawdust Frass**:
   - Early instars (1st–2nd) scrape green chlorophyll, creating translucent parchment-like "window panes" and pinholes.
   - Older instars (3rd–6th) chew large ragged chunks from central whorl leaves.
   - The central funnel becomes heavily clogged with wet, coarse, brown **sawdust-like excreta (*frass / लकड़ी का बुरादा जैसा मल*)**.

## Lookalike Triage: FAW vs. Stem Borer vs. True Armyworm
| Diagnostic Marker | Fall Armyworm (*Spodoptera frugiperda*) | Maize Stem Borer (*Chilo partellus*) | True Armyworm (*Mythimna separata*) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Head Markings** | Inverted 'Y' shape prominently visible | Uniform dark brown head; no inverted 'Y' | Reticulated honey-comb pattern on head |
| **Tail Segment (8th)** | **4 dark spots in a perfect square** | Scattered dark warts; no square pattern | Longitudinal pale and dark stripes |
| **Feeding Pattern** | Ragged tearing with massive moist sawdust frass inside whorl | Clean, regular parallel horizontal rows of shot holes across leaf blade | Ragged defoliation strictly from outer leaf margins inwards |

## Recommended Action & IPM Management Protocols

### 1. Preventive & Non-Chemical Cultural Practices
- **Deep Summer Ploughing**: Turn soil thoroughly in May–June to expose soil-dwelling pupae to intense solar heat and predatory birds (myna, egrets).
- **Certified Seed Treatment (MANDATORY)**:
  - Treat seed with **Cyantraniliprole 19.8% + Thiamethoxam 19.8% FS** @ 6.0 ml per kg seed. This systemic seed dressing prevents FAW egg hatching and early seedling feeding for the first 15–20 days.
- **Intercropping**: Intercrop maize with pulses like Cowpea (*लोबिया*) or Pigeonpea (*अरहर*) in 2:1 ratio. Intercropping increases predatory spiders, ladybird beetles, and parasitoids.
- **Whorl Sand-Neem Treatment (*देसी गोफ उपचार*)**:
  - In smallholder plots, mix dry river sand or fine wood ash with crushed Neem seed cake in a 9:1 ratio.
  - Drop a small pinch (3 to 5 grams) directly into the central funnel/whorl of each plant at 20–25 DAS.
  - *Why Effective*: The abrasive sand grains damage the soft larval cuticle and obstruct their mouthparts, physically suffocating young larvae hiding inside the whorl without chemical expense.

### 2. Pheromone Monitoring & Biocontrol
- Install Sleeve Pheromone Traps baited with **Spodolure** @ 5 traps per acre at 15 DAS.
- Release egg parasitoids *Trichogramma pretiosum* @ 50,000/acre at weekly intervals when moth catches are observed.
- Spray entomopathogenic fungi *Metarhizium rileyi* (formerly *Nomuraea rileyi*) @ 3.0 g/litre or *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) @ 2.0 g/litre.

### 3. Chemical Control Protocols (CIBRC / University Label Verified)
- **Economic Threshold Level (ETL)**:
  - Seedling to Early Whorl (up to 30 DAS): 5% to 10% damaged plants.
  - Mid-Whorl to Tasseling (30–50 DAS): 10% to 20% damaged plants.
- **MANDATORY APPLICATION METHOD**:
  - Spray must be directed **straight downwards into the funnel/whorl of each plant** using a knapsack sprayer with nozzle swirl-plate removed. Broadcasting fine mist over outer leaf surfaces is useless because larvae are buried deep beneath frass in the central whorl.
  - Spray either early morning (before 9:00 AM) or late afternoon (after 4:00 PM) when larvae are actively feeding near the whorl top.
- **Recommended Insecticides**:
  1. **Emamectin Benzoate 5% SG (Proclaim)** @ 0.4 g per litre of water (80 g in 200 L water per acre). *(First choice for high efficacy and low mammalian toxicity).*
  2. **Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC (Coragen)** @ 0.4 ml per litre of water (80 ml in 200 L water per acre).
  3. **Spinetoram 11.7% SC** @ 0.5 ml per litre of water (100 ml in 200 L water per acre).

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Makka me fall armyworm par upar se spray karne par keeda kyu nahi marta? (Why does surface foliar spraying fail against FAW?)**  
  *A*: FAW caterpillars reside deep inside the tight central whorl (funnel), protected beneath a thick plug of sawdust-like droppings (*frass*). A general mist spray never touches the larva. You must remove the nozzle swirl or hold the lance directly over each funnel so the pesticide solution pours straight into the central whorl.
- **Q: Makka me fall armyworm ki pehchan ke 2 mukhya nishan kya hain? (What are the 2 key identification marks of FAW?)**  
  *A*: 1) A clear white inverted 'Y' shape on the front of the dark head. 2) Four distinct black spots arranged in a perfect square on the 8th segment near the tail end.
