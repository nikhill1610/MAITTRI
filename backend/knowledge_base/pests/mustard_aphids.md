# Mustard Aphid (Lipaphis erysimi) Identification & Management (सरसों का चेपा / माहू)

## Farmer-friendly summary
The Mustard Aphid (*Lipaphis erysimi / सरसों का चेपा, माहू*), a tiny, soft-bodied, sap-sucking insect, is the most destructive pest of rapeseed-mustard across Uttar Pradesh and Northern India. During the cloudy, humid, and cool periods of late December through February, millions of winged and wingless aphids multiply exponentially, smothering flower buds, inflorescences, and developing pods (*सिलिकुआ*). Aphids suck plant sap and excrete sweet, sticky **honeydew (*चिपचिपा लासा*)**, which attracts black sooty mold (*काली फफूंद*), blinding the leaves from sunlight. Pods fail to fill, and seeds become shriveled, causing direct yield losses between 30% and 80%.

## When this guidance applies
- **High-Risk Geography**: Western UP (Agra, Mathura, Aligarh, Hathras, Bulandshahr), Central UP (Kanpur, Lucknow), and Bundelkhand.
- **Critical Attack Period**: Late December to mid-February (coinciding with flowering and podding).
- **Environmental Triggers**:
  - Cool temperatures (10°C to 18°C).
  - High relative humidity (>70%), overcast skies, persistent morning fog, and absence of rain.
  - Mild south-westerly winds that carry winged aphid swarms into the crop.

## Identification & Diagnostic Symptoms
1. **Infestation Pattern**:
   - Tiny (1.5 to 2.5 mm long), greenish-yellow to dark green, pear-shaped insects clustering in dense masses on the **tender apical shoots, flowering twigs, and young pods**.
   - Both nymphs and adult females possess a pair of tube-like structures called **cornicles** protruding from the posterior abdomen.
2. **Plant Damage**:
   - Continuous sap drainage causes flowers to dry, wither, and drop prematurely.
   - Developing pods become curled, stunted, and chlorotic with pale yellow streaks.
3. **Honeydew & Sooty Mold (*काली फफूंद*)**:
   - Dense aphid colonies excrete copiously sticky liquid (honeydew).
   - Saprophytic black fungus (*Capnodium brassicae*) grows on the honeydew, coating leaves and pods with a black sooty layer that halts photosynthesis.

## Economic Threshold Level (ETL)
Do **NOT** spray at the first sight of isolated aphids on field borders. Beneficial predators (ladybirds) naturally manage low populations.
- **Official ETL Criterion**: Initiate chemical intervention only when:
  - An aphid colony measuring **1.5 to 2.0 cm in length** is observed on the central flowering twig of **20% of randomly sampled plants** across the field, OR
  - An average population of **50 aphids per plant** is recorded.

## THE CRITICAL HONEYBEE / POLLINATOR SAFETY RULE
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Mustard is an Entomophilous (Insect-Pollinated) Crop**: Honeybees (*Apis mellifera* and *Apis cerana*) are responsible for up to 25% of mustard pollination, directly driving pod set and seed oil yield.
> 
> **MANDATORY SPRAY TIMING**:
> - **NEVER SPRAY CHEMICAL INSECTICIDES IN THE MORNING OR MIDDAY**. Honeybees actively forage between 8:00 AM and 2:30 PM.
> - **STRICTLY SPRAY IN THE LATE AFTERNOON (AFTER 3:30 PM)**. By 3:30 PM, honeybees return to their hives, allowing the insecticide to dry on plant surfaces overnight before bees resume foraging the following day.

## Recommended Action & IPM Management Protocols

### 1. Cultural & Preventative Practices
- **Early Timely Sowing (Crucial Escape Mechanism)**:
  - Sow mustard timely between **October 1 and October 20**.
  - *Why Effective*: Timely sown mustard completes flowering and pod hardening before the January aphid population explosion. Late-sown crops (sown after Nov 5) suffer 3 to 4 times higher damage.
- **Nitrogen Discipline**:
  - Avoid excessive applications of Urea. High Nitrogen produces soft, succulent green shoots with high amino acid content, attracting 5 times more aphids than balanced fertilized fields.
- **Manual Clipping of Initial Infested Twigs (*शुरुआती टहनियों को तोड़ना*)**:
  - In late December, inspect field borders. Clip and destroy the very first aphid-colonized twigs in a polythene bag before winged alates disperse across the field.

### 2. Biological Control & Natural Predator Conservation
- **Conserve Coccinellid Ladybird Beetles (*लेडीबर्ड बीटल / दाल कीड़ा*)**:
  - Adults (*Coccinella septempunctata*) are bright orange/red with black spots.
  - A single adult beetle and its grub consume 40 to 60 aphids per day.
  - If you observe more than **1 ladybird beetle per plant**, delay chemical spraying; biological control will naturally suppress the aphid colony.
- **Botanical Spray**: Spray Neem seed kernel extract (NSKE 5%) or Neem oil (10,000 ppm @ 2 ml/litre) at initial appearance to repel incoming aphid swarms.

### 3. Chemical Control Protocol (Strictly CIBRC / University Label Verified)
When aphid populations cross the 1.5–2.0 cm ETL threshold on 20% plants:
- **Spraying Schedule (Strictly Late Afternoon)**:
  1. **Dimethoate 30% EC (Rogor)** @ 1.0 ml per litre of water (200 ml in 200 L water per acre). *(Systemic organophosphate with rapid knockdown).*
  2. **Thiamethoxam 25% WG (Actara)** @ 0.2 to 0.25 g per litre of water (40–50 g in 200 L water per acre). *(Modern neonicotinoid with prolonged systemic control).*
  3. **Imidacloprid 17.8% SL** @ 0.3 ml per litre of water (60 ml in 200 L water per acre).
- **Spray Volume**: Ensure at least 200 litres of water per acre. Adjust nozzle to wet terminal shoots and inflorescences thoroughly.

## Farmer Decision Table
| Field Observation | Diagnostic Status | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| A few green aphids on 2–3 plants along field borders; ladybird beetles active | Below ETL / Natural Control | Do not spray chemicals. Conserve ladybird beetles; scout again in 4 days. |
| Aphid colonies covering 2 cm of terminal twigs on 1 in every 5 sampled plants | ETL Reached (Economic threshold) | Apply Thiamethoxam 25% WG (0.2 g/L) or Dimethoate (1 ml/L) **strictly after 3:30 PM**. |
| Leaves coated in sticky liquid with black sooty mold; crop in mid-pod stage | Severe Late Infestation | Spray Dimethoate 30% EC (1 ml/L) in late afternoon. Repeat after 10 days if foggy conditions persist. |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Sarson me mahu ki dawa subah kyu nahi chhidakni chahiye? (Why should aphid spray not be applied in the morning?)**  
  *A*: Honeybees visit mustard flowers for nectar and pollen actively during morning and midday hours. Spraying insecticides in the morning kills pollinating bees, destroying pod set and causing up to 25% yield loss. Always spray after 3:30 PM.
- **Q: Sarson me chepa aane ka sabse mukhya mausam kab hota hai? (When is peak aphid attack season in mustard?)**  
  *A*: From late December through January, especially when the weather is cloudy, overcast, foggy, and cool (10–18°C). Bright sunny days naturally suppress aphid reproduction.
