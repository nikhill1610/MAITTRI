# Gram Pod Borer (Helicoverpa armigera) in Pulses: IPM Decision Guide (चने का घेंटी छेदक कीट)

## Farmer-friendly summary
The Gram Pod Borer (*Helicoverpa armigera / चने का घेंटी छेदक*), a voracious caterpillar of a noctuid moth, is the single most destructive pest of chickpea (*चना*) across Uttar Pradesh, with legendary devastation in the Bundelkhand pulse bowl (Banda, Hamirpur, Mahoba, Jalaun, Jhansi). While young caterpillars feed on tender leaves, the real catastrophe occurs during pod development (*घेंटी बनने की अवस्था*), when the caterpillar bores neat **circular holes into green pods and feeds on developing seeds with its head thrust inside the pod and the rest of its body hanging outside**. A single caterpillar can damage 30 to 40 pods across its lifespan, destroying 25% to 60% of grain yield if timely IPM is neglected.

## When this guidance applies
- **High-Risk Geography**: Bundelkhand region and pulse-growing belts of Central and Western UP.
- **Critical Attack Stages**:
  - *Vegetative / Flower Bud Stage (40–55 DAS)*: Early foliage feeding.
  - *Pod Formation & Seed Filling (65–90 DAS)*: **Most critical economic damage window**.
- **Environmental Triggers**:
  - Warm, sunny days following a cool, humid winter spell.
  - Intermittent winter showers in January/February that trigger rapid adult moth emergence.

## Identification & Diagnostic Symptoms
1. **The Caterpillar (*सुंडी*)**:
   - Color is highly variable: ranges from apple-green, olive-green, yellowish, to dark brown with prominent pale and dark longitudinal stripes running down the sides of the body.
   - Body is scattered with tiny short bristles/hairs.
   - Full-grown larva measures 35 to 45 mm in length.
2. **The Signature Feeding Position**:
   - The caterpillar chews a **clean, circular entry hole** into the green pod wall.
   - It inserts only the front half of its head and body inside the hole to consume the developing grain, while the rear half of its body remains resting **outside the pod**.
   - Plump green pods are hollowed out, leaving only an empty papery shell containing dark larval droppings.

## Economic Threshold Level (ETL)
Do **NOT** wait for severe pod damage before taking action:
- **Official ETL Criterion**:
  - **1 to 2 caterpillars per meter of crop row**, OR
  - **5% pod damage** across randomly inspected plants in the field.

## Recommended Action & IPM Management Protocols

### 1. Cultural & Ecological Engineering Practices
- **Deep Summer Ploughing**: Turn soil deeply in May–June to expose subterranean pupae to predatory birds and intense solar sterilization.
- **T-Shaped Bird Perches (*T-आकार के पक्षी बसेरे*)**:
  - Install 20 to 25 wooden or bamboo **'T'-shaped bird perches** per acre across the chickpea field when the crop is 35–40 days old (approx. 1 meter high, standing just above the crop canopy).
  - *Why Highly Effective*: Insectivorous native birds (Black Drongo / *कोतवाल / भुजंगा*, Common Myna / *मैना*, Indian Roller / *नीलकंठ*) utilize these perches to hunt, consuming hundreds of active Helicoverpa caterpillars daily with zero chemical cost.
  - *Note*: Remove bird perches once pods begin ripening and grain hardens, so birds do not feed on the crop itself.
- **Intercropping & Border Cropping**:
  - Intercrop Chickpea with **Mustard (*सरसों*) or Linseed (*अलसी*)** in a 6:1 or 8:1 ratio.
  - Plant a border row of **Coriander (*धनिया*) or Marigold (*गेंदा*)**. Coriander flowers provide nectar for predatory wasps (*Campoletis sonorensis*), which naturally parasitize young Helicoverpa larvae.

### 2. Pheromone Monitoring & Mass Trapping
- Install Sleeve Pheromone Traps baited with **Helilure** @ 5 to 8 traps per acre at 35–40 DAS.
- Place traps 30 cm above the canopy. Trap catches of >4 to 5 male moths per trap per day for 3 consecutive days signal an egg-laying peak, indicating that tiny larvae will emerge within 4 to 6 days.

### 3. Biological & Microbial Control (Early Instar Window)
When larvae are small (<10 mm, 1st and 2nd instars) and before they bore inside pods:
- **HaNPV (Helicoverpa armigera Nuclear Polyhedrosis Virus)**:
  - Spray HaNPV @ 250 LE (Larval Equivalents) per hectare (100 LE/acre) mixed with 0.1% jaggery (*गुड़* as a feeding stimulant) and 0.1% starch.
  - **Strictly spray in the evening** because ultraviolet sunlight degrades the virus.
- **Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt)**: Spray *Bacillus thuringiensis* kurstaki @ 2.0 g/litre.
- **Botanical Spray**: Spray 5% Neem Seed Kernel Extract (NSKE) or Neem oil (10,000 ppm @ 2.0 ml/litre) at 50% flowering.

### 4. Chemical Control Protocols (Strictly CIBRC / University Label Verified)
When larval populations exceed the ETL of 1–2 larvae/meter row during active podding:
- **Spraying Protocol**: Use a knapsack sprayer with hollow cone nozzle with at least 200 litres of water per acre. Ensure thorough canopy penetration.
- **Recommended Insecticides**:
  1. **Emamectin Benzoate 5% SG (Proclaim)** @ 0.4 g per litre of water (80 g in 200 L water per acre). *(First-choice modern avermectin with rapid stomach and contact action).*
  2. **Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC (Coragen)** @ 0.3 ml per litre of water (60 ml in 200 L water per acre).
  3. **Indoxacarb 14.5% SC** @ 1.0 ml per litre of water (200 ml in 200 L water per acre).
  4. **Spinosad 45% SC** @ 0.3 ml per litre of water.

## Farmer Decision Table
| Field Observation | Infestation Phase | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| Crop is 40 days old; vegetative branching; scattered tiny larvae on tender leaves | Early vegetative phase | Erect 20 'T'-shaped bird perches/acre; spray 5% NSKE (Neem extract) or Bt. |
| Helilure pheromone traps catch >5 moths/day for 3 days; flowers emerging | Egg hatching peak | Spray HaNPV (250 LE/ha) in late evening with jaggery water. |
| Green pods have circular holes; 1–2 caterpillars found per meter row | ETL crossed at podding | Immediately apply Emamectin Benzoate 5% SG (0.4 g/L) or Chlorantraniliprole (0.3 ml/L). |
| Hardened mature pods; crop ready for harvest in 10 days | Crop maturity phase | Do not spray chemicals. Remove bird perches and plan harvest on a clear sunny day. |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Chane ke khet me 'T' aakar ki lakdiyan (bird perches) lagane ka kya fayda hai? (What is the benefit of 'T'-shaped bird perches in chickpea?)**  
  *A*: Native birds like Black Drongo (*कोतवाल*) and Myna need a stable perch to spot caterpillars. Erecting 20–25 'T' perches per acre gives them hunting stations right above the crop canopy. The birds pick and eat hundreds of pod borer caterpillars every day for free.
- **Q: Chane me ghenti chedaka ki dawa kab chhidakni chahiye? (When should pod borer spray be applied in chickpea?)**  
  *A*: Spray when pod formation begins and you observe 1 to 2 caterpillars per meter of crop row. Waiting until pods are already hollowed out with circular holes is too late. Spray Emamectin Benzoate (80 g/acre) in 200 litres of water.
