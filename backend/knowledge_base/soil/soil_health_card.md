# Soil Health Card (SHC) Interpretation & Testing Protocol (मृदा स्वास्थ्य कार्ड एवं मिट्टी परीक्षण विधि)

## Farmer-friendly summary
The **Soil Health Card (SHC / *मृदा स्वास्थ्य कार्ड*)** is an official Government of India diagnostic report issued to farmers every 3 years. It evaluates the exact fertility status of farm soil across **12 critical chemical and physical parameters** and provides crop-specific, customized fertilizer recommendations. Transitioning from generic blanket fertilizer guessing to **Soil-Test-Based Targeted Yield prescriptions** saves ₹1,200 to ₹2,500 per acre by eliminating unnecessary fertilizer waste, rectifying hidden micronutrient deficiencies (like Zinc or Sulphur), and restoring damaged soil biology. A reliable card begins with an accurate soil sample; improper sampling invalidates the entire test.

## The 12 Mandatory Soil Health Parameters
The official Soil Health Card tests and reports 12 distinct soil properties divided into 4 scientific categories:

1. **Macronutrients (Primary Nutrients - 3)**:
   - Available Nitrogen ($N$) — measured in kg/ha.
   - Available Phosphorus ($P$ / $P_2O_5$) — measured in kg/ha.
   - Available Potassium ($K$ / $K_2O$) — measured in kg/ha.
2. **Secondary Macronutrient (1)**:
   - Available Sulphur ($S$) — measured in parts per million (ppm or mg/kg).
3. **Micronutrients (5)**:
   - Zinc ($Zn$) — DTPA extractable, ppm.
   - Iron ($Fe$) — DTPA extractable, ppm.
   - Copper ($Cu$) — DTPA extractable, ppm.
   - Manganese ($Mn$) — DTPA extractable, ppm.
   - Boron ($B$) — Hot water soluble, ppm.
4. **Physical & Reaction Parameters (3)**:
   - **pH (Soil Reaction)**: Measures acidity, neutrality, or alkalinity.
   - **EC (Electrical Conductivity)**: Measures total soluble salts (salinity hazard), in deciSiemens per meter (dS/m).
   - **Organic Carbon (OC)**: Measured as percentage (%), indicating soil organic matter content and biological life.

## Standard Fertility Rating Ranges & Interpretation Matrix
| Parameter | Low Range (Apply +25% Dose) | Medium Range (Apply 100% Rec. Dose) | High Range (Reduce Dose by 25%) | Agronomic Significance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Organic Carbon (OC)** | **< 0.50%** | **0.50% – 0.75%** | **> 0.75%** | Key index of microbial activity and nitrogen-supplying power. |
| **Available Nitrogen (N)** | < 280 kg/ha | 280 – 560 kg/ha | > 560 kg/ha | Governs vegetative vigor; split urea accordingly. |
| **Available Phosphorus (P)** | < 10 kg/ha (P) / < 23 kg P2O5 | 10 – 25 kg/ha (P) | > 25 kg/ha (P) | Essential for root establishment; place basally 5 cm deep. |
| **Available Potassium (K)** | < 110 kg/ha (K) / < 133 kg K2O | 110 – 280 kg/ha (K) | > 280 kg/ha (K) | Lodging resistance, water regulation, and starch quality. |
| **Available Sulphur (S)** | < 10 ppm | 10 – 20 ppm | > 20 ppm | Mandatory for mustard oil synthesis and pulse proteins. |
| **Available Zinc (Zn)** | **< 0.60 ppm** | **0.60 – 1.20 ppm** | **> 1.20 ppm** | <0.6 ppm triggers Khaira in paddy and White Bud in maize. |
| **Available Iron (Fe)** | < 4.5 ppm | 4.5 – 9.0 ppm | > 9.0 ppm | Interveinal chlorosis in high-pH calcareous soils. |
| **Soil pH** | < 6.5 (Acidic) | **6.5 – 7.5 (Optimal Neutral)** | > 8.5 (Alkaline / Sodic Usar) | Governs nutrient availability; >8.5 locks up zinc and P. |
| **Electrical Cond. (EC)** | **< 1.0 dS/m (Normal)** | 1.0 – 2.0 dS/m (Sensitive crops hurt) | > 2.0 dS/m (Saline Hazard) | High EC causes osmotic drought; requires drainage leaching. |

## Scientific Soil Sampling Protocol: The "V"-Notch Method

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A SOIL TEST IS ONLY AS GOOD AS THE SOIL SAMPLE**:
> Over 80% of testing errors originate from incorrect field sampling. Follow this strict 6-step ICAR protocol:
> 
> 1. **Timing**: Sample post-harvest, after previous crop residues are cleared, before sowing the next crop.
> 2. **Sampling Depth**:
>    - For Field Crops (Wheat, Rice, Mustard, Pulses): **0 to 15 cm depth** (active root plough layer).
>    - For Deep-Rooted Crops / Horticulture (Fruit trees): **0 to 30 cm and 30 to 60 cm depths**.
> 3. **The 'V'-Shape Cut**:
>    - Clear surface organic litter.
>    - Dig a 'V'-shaped pit 15 cm deep with a clean spade (*फावड़ा*).
>    - Cut a uniform **2 to 3 cm thick slice of soil** along the exposed side of the 'V'-notch from top to bottom.
> 4. **Zig-Zag Composite Sampling**:
>    - Collect slices from **8 to 12 random spots** in a zig-zag path across each uniform 1-hectare field.
> 5. **Places Strictly to Avoid**:
>    - Never sample near field bunds, compost heaps, under tree shade, cattle shades, or along irrigation channels.
> 6. **The Quartering Method (*चौथाईकरण विधि*)**:
>    - Mix all 10 composite samples thoroughly in a clean plastic bucket.
>    - Spread soil on a clean plastic sheet; divide into 4 equal quarters.
>    - Discard two diagonally opposite quarters. Re-mix the remaining soil.
>    - Repeat until exactly **500 grams of clean soil** remains. Pack in a cloth bag with farmer name, village, plot Khasra number, and date.

## Adjusting Fertilizer Applications based on Soil Health Card
- **If Nutrient is LOW (<280 kg N or <10 kg P)**:
  - **Increase recommended fertilizer dose by 25%** to rebuild depleted soil nutrient reserves.
- **If Nutrient is MEDIUM (Standard range)**:
  - Apply exactly **100% of the State University Recommended Dose (RDF)**.
- **If Nutrient is HIGH (>560 kg N or >25 kg P)**:
  - **Reduce fertilizer dose by 25%**. Applying full fertilizer to soils already rich in nutrients leads to runoff, lodging, and economic waste.

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Mrida Swasthya Card kitne saal me dobara banwana chahiye? (How often should Soil Health Card be renewed?)**  
  *A*: Every **3 years**. Dynamic soil parameters (Nitrogen, Zinc, Sulphur) change rapidly under continuous intensive double-cropping. Re-testing every 3 years ensures balanced fertilization.
- **Q: Khet me se kitni jagah se mitti leni chahiye? (From how many spots should soil be collected?)**  
  *A*: For a uniform 1-hectare (2.5 acre) field, collect from **8 to 10 random spots** in a zig-zag pattern across the plot. Mix them together and use the quartering method to prepare a single 500-gram sample.
