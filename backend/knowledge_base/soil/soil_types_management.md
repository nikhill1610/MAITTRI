# Major Soil Types of North India & Management Practices (उत्तर प्रदेश की प्रमुख मृदाएं एवं उनका प्रबंधन)

## Farmer-friendly summary
Uttar Pradesh encompasses two fundamentally distinct geographic and geological realms: the vast, deep, fertile **Indo-Gangetic Alluvial Plains** (covering >85% of the state) and the rocky, undulating **Bundelkhand & Vindhyan Plateau** in the south. Soil texture, clay mineralogy, and water-retention capacity vary profoundly between these regions. A farming practice suited for the loamy alluvial soils of Meerut or Varanasi will fail completely on the expanding black clay soils (*मार / काबर*) or gravelly red soils (*राकर / पड़वा*) of Bundelkhand. Understanding your regional soil classification unlocks targeted tillage, precision crop selection, and water-conserving soil management.

---

## 1. The Indo-Gangetic Alluvial Soils (*जलोढ़ मृदा*)

Formed by deposition of silt, clay, and sand brought down from the Himalayas by the Ganga, Yamuna, Ghaghara, and Gomti river systems. Deep, flat, fertile, and responsive to irrigation.

### Sub-Types of Alluvial Soils:
1. **Khadar Soils (*खादर - नवीन जलोढ़ / New Alluvium*)**:
   - **Location**: Low-lying floodplains immediately adjacent to riverbanks, annually renewed by monsoon flood silts.
   - **Texture & Properties**: Silty loam to fine sandy loam; light grey to ash-colored; well-aerated; high water table.
   - **Best Suited Crops**: Cucurbits (Watermelon, Muskmelon, Bottle Gourd), Sugarcane, Maize, Rice, Potato, and Summer vegetables.
   - **Management**: Prone to monsoon flooding. Focus on short-duration pre-flood crops (Zaid crops) and post-monsoon Rabi sowing.
2. **Bhangar Soils (*बांगर - प्राचीन जलोढ़ / Old Alluvium*)**:
   - **Location**: Higher river terraces located above the flood line, covering the vast agricultural heartland of Western, Central, and Eastern UP.
   - **Texture & Properties**: Heavier texture (loam to clay loam); darker brown color; contains sub-surface calcium carbonate nodules (*कंकड़ / Kankar pan*).
   - **Best Suited Crops**: The world's most productive **Rice-Wheat rotation**, Sugarcane, Mustard, Potato, and Maize.
   - **Management Risk**: Sub-surface plough pans (*रोटावेटर से बनी सख्त परत*) form easily due to repetitive rotavator tillage at the same 10 cm depth. Run a sub-soiler or chisel plough once every 3 years to break the hardpan. Prone to sodic Usar development if drainage is neglected.

## 2. Bundelkhand & Vindhyan Soils (Southern UP)

Bundelkhand (Jhansi, Lalitpur, Jalaun, Hamirpur, Mahoba, Banda, Chitrakoot) features ancient pre-Cambrian crystalline geology. Soils are locally classified into two major families: **Black Soils (*काली मृदा*)** and **Red Soils (*लाल मृदा*)**.

### A. Black Soil Series: Mar & Kabar (*मार एवं काबर*)
1. **Mar (*मार*)**:
   - **Properties**: Deep black, highly clayey soil (montmorillonite clay >45%). Rich in organic matter, calcium, and iron, but deficient in phosphorus and nitrogen.
   - **The "Self-Ploughing" Property**: High swelling when wet and shrinking when dry. In hot summers, deep, wide vertical cracks (50–100 cm deep) develop, allowing surface soil to fall inside (*स्वतः जुताई*).
   - **Extreme Tillage Constraint**:
     - When wet: Becomes exceptionally sticky, plastic, and unworkable. Heavy tractor wheels sink and slip.
     - When dry: Bakes into rock-hard clods that cannot be tilled.
     - **The "Ot" Window (*ओट का महत्व*)**: Ploughing and sowing must strictly be performed during the narrow moisture window immediately following rain before the soil either dries or becomes muddy.
   - **Best Suited Crops**: Chickpea (*चना*), Lentil (*मसूर*), Wheat, Soybean, and Mustard.
2. **Kabar (*काबर*)**:
   - Similar to Mar, but lighter in color (dark greyish-brown to black) with slightly higher sand/silt content. Less plastic than Mar, but highly prone to surface crusting after light winter showers.

### B. Red Soil Series: Parwa & Rakar (*पड़वा एवं राकर*)
1. **Parwa (*पड़वा*)**:
   - **Properties**: Light reddish-brown to greyish-red sandy loam to loam. Found on higher undulating plains. Moderately deep, permeable, and easy to cultivate across a wide moisture range.
   - **Best Suited Crops**: Pigeonpea (*अरहर*), Sesame (*तिल*), Mustard, Wheat (under sprinkler irrigation), and Sorghum.
   - **Management**: Moderate water-holding capacity. Responds strongly to organic manuring (FYM) and balanced NPK fertilization.
2. **Rakar (*राकर*)**:
   - **Properties**: True red, shallow, gravelly, highly eroded soil found on hill slopes and ravines. Extremely poor water-holding capacity, low organic matter, and rapid nutrient leaching.
   - **Best Suited Crops**: Hardy, drought-tolerant coarse grains and pulses: Pearl Millet (*बाजरा*), Small Millets (*कोदो, कुटकी*), Sesame (*तिल*), Horsegram (*कुलथी*).
   - **Rehabilitation Strategy**: Construct contour bunds and crescent trenches to arrest rainwater runoff. Practice agroforestry (Aonla, Ber, Subabul) to build soil organic carbon.

## Comprehensive Soil Management Matrix
| Soil Type | Key Constraint | Best Suited Crops | Priority Tillage & Management Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alluvial Loam (Bhangar)** | Sub-surface hardpan; declining organic carbon | Rice, Wheat, Sugarcane, Potato | Chisel plough every 3 years; incorporate crop residue (Super Seeder); apply green manure. |
| **Alluvial Sandy Loam (Khadar)** | High leaching; low water holding capacity | Maize, Mustard, Vegetables, Melons | Frequent light irrigations; split urea into 3–4 applications; add farmyard manure. |
| **Black Soil (Mar / Kabar)** | Narrow tillage window; severe water stagnation | Chickpea, Lentil, Wheat, Soybean | Broad Bed & Furrow (BBF) system; sow strictly at proper soil moisture (*ओट*); avoid over-irrigation. |
| **Red Gravelly Soil (Rakar)**| Shallow depth; rapid drought drying | Millets (Bajra, Kodo), Sesame, Pulses | Contour bunding; mulching; heavy compost additions; drought-hardy legumes. |

## Farmer Decision Table
| Field Situation | Recommended Soil Strategy |
|---|---|
| Black soil in Bundelkhand turning too sticky after heavy rain | Wait 3–4 days until soil moisture reaches workable friable consistency (*ओट*) before running tractor. |
| Sandy loam field drying out rapidly within 4 days of watering | Add 4 tons/acre FYM or vermicompost; apply organic straw mulch to cut surface evaporation by 40%. |
| Clay loam field where water stands on surface after 1st wheat irrigation | Run a deep chisel plough post-harvest to shatter the sub-surface hardpan and restore natural percolation. |
| Undulating sloping land prone to soil erosion during monsoon | Construct contour bunds along slope lines and plant Vetiver (*खस*) grass along borders to trap topsoil. |

## Common Farmer Questions & Answers (FAQ)
- **Q: Bundelkhand ki mar aur kabar mitti me chana sabse accha kyu hota hai? (Why does chickpea perform best in Bundelkhand black soil?)**  
  *A*: Mar and Kabar black soils have exceptionally high clay content that retains residual monsoon moisture deep in the subsoil profile throughout the dry winter months. Deep-rooted chickpea tap roots extract this stored subsoil water, producing bumper grain yields without needing frequent tube-well irrigations.
- **Q: Khet me bar-bar rotavator chalane se mitti par kya bura asar padta hai? (What is the negative impact of repeated rotavator use?)**  
  *A*: Rotavators pulverize natural soil aggregates into fine dust and create a hard, compressed impermeable layer (*कड़ा पैन / hardpan*) at 10–12 cm depth. This prevents crop roots from growing deeper and causes severe waterlogging during rain. Use a cultivator or mouldboard plough periodically instead.
